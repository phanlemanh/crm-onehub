====================================================================================================
Retrieval architecture: LLM Wiki vs RAG vs GraphRAG vs hybrid vs long-context, for a corpus of tens to a few hundred policy documents on Postgres
--- SUMMARY
The evidence does not support choosing between "wiki" and "RAG" as rival architectures at OneHub's scale; it supports a wiki as the content layer with a cheap search tool on top. The best controlled comparison found (Cochran, preregistered, 24 papers, 13 questions) shows the compiled wiki with an agent that lists and reads pages beats a strong hybrid+rerank RAG on cross-document synthesis and on claim-level citation support (40.2% vs 18.9% of cited claims fully supported). The same paper found the wiki agent used about 21x more query tokens and about 6.6x more wall-clock time, and that a decomposition-retrieval RAG variant closed most of the synthesis gap but not the citation-support gap. For small corpora, agent-driven exact-match search (grep or BM25) is repeatedly competitive with or better than vector retrieval: at about 1,100 documents an LLM agent exploring files scored 77.4% vs BM25 74.7%, and the crossover to BM25 came near 10M corpus tokens; Claude Code's team reports dropping a vector DB for agentic search, but that is a code-search claim. Index-only navigation is reported to work to about 100 sources or a few hundred pages, but a 709-page wiki still worked because capable agents skipped the index and searched, while practitioners report failure at 500 to several thousand pages, so a search tool is the cheap insurance. GraphRAG is the weakest fit: it targets global sensemaking over roughly 1M-token corpora, frequently underperforms vanilla RAG on single-hop questions, and costs 41x more to build in one benchmark, so wikilinks plus link-following already give the multi-hop benefit you would want. Long-context stuffing is feasible at this scale (1M-token windows at standard pricing, cache reads at 0.1x) and can even be more accurate in some studies, but context rot, per-query cost and cache invalidation on every approved edit make it a poor default for the employee Q&A path. Postgres-native options are sufficient: tsvector plus unaccent plus pg_trgm for lexical, pgvector with exact scan for semantic, fused with RRF; ts_rank has no IDF, and BM25 (lakebase_text on Neon) is a drop-in upgrade if needed. On citing and refusing, the evidence is sobering: models often answer instead of abstaining when context is insufficient (0 of 5 correct abstentions across four hosted RAG services on an 81-document corpus; AbstentionBench reports reasoning fine-tuning degrades abstention by 24% on average), so refusal and quote-level citation should be enforced by deterministic checks, not prompt wording alone. Most evidence is on papers, transcripts, personal notes and synthetic enterprise data, not human-approved policy wikis in Vietnamese, and my web-search budget ran out before I could finish Vietnamese BM25-vs-dense checks.
--- TENSIONS
 * Wiki vs RAG on synthesis: the wiki wins cross-paper synthesis in Cochran's study, but a decomposition-RAG variant recovered about 88% of that gap at 3.4x lower cost, and once both judges' scores were combined the structural-integrity advantage fell below the preregistered threshold. The gap that did not close was claim-level citation support (40.2% vs 19.2%).
 * Cost thesis contradicted: Karpathy's framing and many blogs say compile-once makes queries cheaper, but the only preregistered test found the wiki agent used about 21x more tokens per query (with max reasoning effort); another paper (ISC) claims the opposite (2.2K vs 16.3K reader tokens) for compiled claims plus embeddings, on interview transcripts. The difference is agent-browses-pages vs precompiled claims served by retrieval.
 * Where index-first navigation breaks: 'about 100 pages' (gist and commenters), 500+ (davidmpinto), thousands (bitsofchris, GitHub issue at 1,690 pages), yet a 709-page wiki worked because capable agents ignored the index and searched. The disagreement is partly about whether the catalog is loaded whole or the agent searches.
 * Grep/BM25 vs vector: Claude Code's team and the grep paper favour lexical agentic search; the same grep paper shows delivery path can flip the winner, the Vietnamese cross-lingual paper shows dense far ahead when query and text languages differ, and Anthropic's contextual retrieval shows hybrid beating either. Domain (code and literal recall vs paraphrase and cross-lingual) explains much of it.
 * Long context vs RAG: Self-Route and Token Tax find long-context more accurate (73.1% vs 65.4%) at 26x tokens; Chroma and NoLiMa show degradation with length; Anthropic's 2024 advice to stuff under 200K tokens predates 1M windows and cheap cache reads. Studies use different model generations and tasks, so none maps cleanly onto a 2026 model with Vietnamese policy pages.
 * GraphRAG: promoted for global questions and cheaper variants (LazyGraphRAG), but multiple benchmarks find it below vanilla RAG on single-hop and detail questions, and graph quality depends on the extraction model. Hybrid selection or integration is the consistent recommendation.
 * Wiki as derived artifact: proponents say compile once and keep the human-reviewed layer authoritative; critics (frosk1, HN commenters, one Medium piece titled as a 'bad idea' that I could not open) say summarisation loses nuance, errors compound and the wiki must not replace raw-source grounding. OneHub's human-approval diff addresses compounding but not paraphrase loss.
 * Evaluation validity: rubric groundedness and claim-level support disagree in direction; Gemini ceilinged at 10/10; the progressive-disclosure study's reliability audit failed (kappa 0.23); the GraphRAG summarisation comparisons flip with ordering. Headline scores from LLM judges should be treated cautiously.
 * Vendor incentives: Atlan (data catalog), ParadeDB and Tiger Data (Postgres search extensions), LlamaIndex (retrieval), Neon/Databricks (lakebase search) each frame the problem toward their product; qmd and OMEGA report self-run benchmarks.
--- IMPLICATIONS
 > Build the wiki as the content layer and give the agent two read-only tools over approved pages only: search_pages(query) and read_page(slug). Back search with Postgres tsvector (websearch_to_tsquery) plus unaccent and pg_trgm; add a compact catalog (title, one-line summary, owner, effective date) the agent can read first. Do not start with a vector pipeline or GraphRAG. Evidence: agent plus ranked lexical search is the strongest scalable pattern; a 709-page wiki worked with a catalog and capable agents skipped it; GraphRAG costs 41x more to build and often loses on single-hop questions.
 > Treat pgvector as a measured phase-2 addition: at hundreds of pages, exact scan needs no ANN index; fuse with RRF (k=60) only if the eval set shows lexical misses on paraphrase and diacritic-free or slang queries. If you store embeddings of 3,072 dimensions use halfvec (indexable to 4,000) or reduced dimensions; test 2-3 multilingual models on 30-50 real Vietnamese questions before choosing, because I found no monolingual Vietnamese benchmark on policy text.
 > Use wikilinks and a 'related pages' frontmatter field as the graph. Link-following gave the multi-hop gains in the LLM-Wiki paper (progressive traversal +11.7-13.8 F1); you get most of what GraphRAG offers for free from the human-curated links and the lint check for orphans and contradictions.
 > Enforce 'approved pages only' in SQL (status and current-revision filter, ideally a view the agent's tools can see), not in the prompt. Generate the tsvector from the approved revision so lexical freshness is transactional; queue embedding refresh on approval.
 > Make pages quote-preserving. Cochran's wiki won on claim-level support because pages hold claim-shaped evidence, but practitioners warn compilation paraphrases and drops page/clause numbers, and Karpathy's design cites wiki pages, not sources. For policies the compile step should keep verbatim clause text with the source document, clause number and effective date, so an answer can quote it exactly.
 > Citation contract: every claim carries page slug + revision + a verbatim quote; a server-side check confirms the quote is an exact substring of that approved revision and rejects or retracts claims that fail. If the agent's provider supports Anthropic search_result blocks or Citations, use them as a second guard (valid pointers, free cited_text), remembering they conflict with structured outputs and prove existence not support. Evaluate support at claim level, not with a holistic LLM score (judge agreement rho was 0.04 on groundedness).
 > Refusal contract must be deterministic, not just prompted. Models answer instead of abstaining (0/5 abstention across four hosted RAG services; reasoning models abstain worse). Suggested rule: if search returns nothing above a minimum lexical/semantic score, or no quote passes the substring check, return a fixed 'not covered by approved pages' response that names the closest pages and logs the question to a gap queue for page owners. Seed the eval set with 15-25% unanswerable and near-miss questions.
 > Build a small eval before choosing more machinery: 40-60 real staff questions covering single-fact, multi-page, conflicting-policy, effective-date and unanswerable cases, with human-labelled expected pages. Compare (a) catalog-only navigation, (b) tsvector search tool, (c) hybrid with pgvector, tracking answer accuracy, claim-level citation support, tokens, latency and refusal precision. Both Cochran papers and the grep paper show results move with the harness as much as with the retriever, so measure your own agent loop.
 > Keep long-context stuffing out of the employee Q&A path. It is affordable in 2026 (1M window, flat pricing, 0.1x cache reads) and can be more accurate on small corpora, but every approved edit invalidates the cached prefix, tokens per Vietnamese page are likely higher, and context-rot studies show degradation with length. Reserve it for batch jobs such as lint, contradiction sweeps and ingest-time page updates over a bounded slice.
 > Set a latency and cost budget for the agent loop: Cochran's wiki agent took about 100 s per question at max reasoning effort with 30 turns; the LLM-Wiki paper reports 15-27 s. Cap turns, use moderate reasoning effort, and prefer one search call returning snippets plus two or three page reads over browsing.
 > Extension and hosting notes for Neon (the repo's setup docs name Neon as the live database): pgvector, pg_trgm and unaccent are the safe set; do not plan on pg_search (removed September 21, 2026); if ts_rank proves weak, lakebase_text gives BM25 over the same tsvector column, but its docs show only the English config, so test with a 'simple'+unaccent configuration first. Verify unaccent immutability and Vietnamese (NFC/NFD, double-diacritic) handling with a migration test before relying on it in a generated column.
 > Expected breakpoints for planning: catalog-in-prompt is comfortable to roughly 100-300 pages (a few tens of thousands of tokens); past about 500 pages rely on search; 1,000+ pages the catalog itself should be hierarchical or search-only. Vector RAG and hybrid have no hard break at this size; GraphRAG never pays back here; long-context stuffing breaks on cost and accuracy well before the 1M limit.
