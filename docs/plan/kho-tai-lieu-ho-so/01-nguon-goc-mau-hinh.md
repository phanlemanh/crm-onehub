Origin and core pattern of the LLM Wiki (Karpathy, April 2026)
--- SUMMARY
The LLM Wiki began as a tweet on 2 April 2026 (titled LLM Knowledge Bases, about 22M views). Karpathy followed it two days later, on 4 April, with a gist called an idea file: a short, deliberately abstract markdown document meant to be pasted into your own agent so the agent builds a version for you. The gist has three layers. Raw sources are immutable and human-curated. The wiki is LLM-written markdown that the LLM owns entirely. The schema is a CLAUDE.md or AGENTS.md file that turns the LLM into a disciplined maintainer. The agent performs three named operations: ingest (one source can touch 10-15 pages), query (with good answers filed back as new pages), and lint (health checks for contradictions, stale claims, orphans, missing pages and cross-references, and data gaps). Two navigation files hold it together: a content-oriented index.md that the LLM reads first, and an append-only log.md with greppable prefixes. The core contrast with RAG is timing. The wiki compiles knowledge once at ingest and keeps it current, instead of re-deriving it from chunks on every question. It also avoids embedding infrastructure at moderate scale, roughly 100 sources and a few hundred pages, though the gist itself points to a hybrid search tool (qmd) beyond that. Karpathy's own stated limits are modest: the scale ceiling, image handling, his preference for one-at-a-time supervised ingest, and a wish for a real product instead of a hacky script collection. Across the primary sources and the 1,128-comment gist thread I found no direct reply from him to the epistemic-risk critiques. The term epistemic drift is not his and is not in the gist or its comments; it is secondary-blog vocabulary for a LLM misreading a source at ingest so the error persists and feeds later ingests. The thread's mature answer is review-gated and provenance-first: source-linked claims, content hashes, diffs instead of silent overwrites, and all writes landing through PR-style review. That is close to OneHub's planned design and departs from the gist's personal-use baseline, where the LLM owns the wiki and the human rarely edits it.
--- TENSIONS
 * Who owns the wiki: the gist says the LLM owns the wiki layer entirely and the human rarely edits, but laphilosophia calls that sentence the risky one for team or high-accuracy use, pnakamura puts a human gate before every update, and pradocabrero requires all writes via PR. This is the tension OneHub's page-owner approval resolves.
 * Wiki versus RAG: Karpathy frames the wiki as instead of RAG for chunk retrieval, but his tweet reads as a scale observation (he expected to need RAG and did not at about 100 articles). Critics say this is not a goodbye-to-RAG moment and recommend RAG for grounding, and practitioners at scale add hybrid search anyway (distorx, v2, qmd in the gist itself).
 * Autonomy versus review: v2 has the LLM propose default contradiction resolutions, self-heal lint and use last-write-wins for mesh sync, while theafh surfaces contested pages with both sides quoted and lets the human decide, Glukhov forbids silently blending conflicting claims, and theaioperator lets scheduled agents change things but delays permanence by 24 hours.
 * Mischaracterizations of the gist: theaioperator says the pattern is append-only with existing pages frozen and dates the gist to February 2026. The gist actually says ingest updates entity and concept pages and revises summaries, and it was created 4 April 2026. Secondary posts also say Karpathy uses Claude Code, which the gist does not state.
 * Who the reader is: the gist and Newton treat wiki pages as human-browsable (Obsidian, Wikipedia-style), while theaioperator and iBlinkQ argue notes should be optimized for LLM retrieval with type and summary fields, and Newton found Claude's terse style unreadable and had it rewritten. OneHub has both human and agent readers.
 * Lint versus write-time resolution: Karpathy proposes periodic lint to find contradictions and stale claims; madgodinc and crajah argue conflict resolution belongs at write time because finding contradictions later is costly; SonicBotMan argues for typed frontmatter and deterministic structural invariants that a prose rewrite cannot corrupt.
 * Scale ceilings: about 100 sources (Karpathy), under about 500 articles (Rakuten commenter), 150-200 pages (secondary articles), 1,440 pages (Newton), thousands of notes with hybrid search (distorx), and an unsupported dies-at-10,000 claim. Only the first is first-hand.
 * Where the immutable raw layer lives: Karpathy copies sources into a raw folder; pradocabrero argues copies go stale for living sources and instead pins version references (file@SHA:lines) and reads deterministic distilled derivatives.
 * Cost of compile: proponents say maintenance approaches zero; R&D World found maintenance time roughly equals time saved after a month, Newton reports constant upkeep, and Mehul Gupta argues cost just moves earlier in the pipeline.
 * Human epistemics: Karpathy says the human directs, asks and thinks while the LLM does bookkeeping; the Effortless Academic post argues ingest outsources interpretation and dropped caveats in the author's own papers; iBlinkQ warns that hoarding unreviewed AI output does not build understanding.
 * Is it a wiki at all: commenters (mauceri, redmizt) dispute whether markdown files count as a wiki without permissions, collaborative editing and a database, and some argue markdown lacks foreign keys and access control. Hosted variants (Hjarni, wikibrain) trade git history and filesystem for a database.
 * Vendor framing: Mem0 (memory), Neo4j (graph), Atlan (data catalog), Penfield (memory backend), and Hjarni (hosted) each present their product as what the pattern is missing.
--- IMPLICATIONS
 > Inference: OneHub's planned model (agent drafts, page owner approves every change as a diff, company agent answers only from approved pages) matches what the gist thread's production practitioners converged on (review-gated wiki, all writes via PR, read-only serving, publish as an operator blessing). It departs from the gist's personal baseline where the LLM owns the wiki and the human rarely edits. The gist supports this in one line: possibly with humans in the loop reviewing updates.
 > Inference: map the three layers to OneHub concepts. Raw sources become immutable, versioned Documents (never edited by the agent). The wiki becomes pages with draft and approved versions. The schema becomes owned configuration (page types, required sections, citation policy) that itself changes only through approved amendments, as in pradocabrero's amendment regime. index.md can be a derived catalog of approved pages only, and log.md can be an append-only event or audit table with the same greppable operation prefix idea.
 > Inference: make every claim source-linked at passage level and pin the source version and content hash on each page version, so a changed or superseded regulation marks dependent pages stale mechanically rather than by LLM guesswork (Freelance, theafh, pradocabrero patterns). Treat a claim with no source as a defect that blocks approval, as in the R&D World setup. Regulations and policies have effective dates, so consider separate effective-date and learned-date fields (crajah's valid-time versus belief-time idea).
 > Inference: give the diff review a source-side view. Show the changed sentences next to the cited raw passages, prioritize deletions and status changes (Glukhov: LLMs often delete detail while smoothing prose), and use risk tiers so trivial link and format changes are cheap while canonical-page rewrites, contradiction resolutions and legal or financial claims always need the page owner. This addresses mikhashev's speed asymmetry between generation and review.
 > Inference: the pattern's file-back step (good answers saved as new pages) is where fabrications can become sources. In OneHub, answers from the employee-facing agent should never write to the wiki directly. They should enter only as proposals through the same review gate, and the answering agent should read approved pages only, with citations carrying page version and, ideally, a quote check against the cited text (Cortex pattern).
 > Inference: because Karpathy's own evidence is a single-user wiki of about 100 articles, do not treat index-only navigation as validated for a company corpus. Plan for search beyond the index (his own gist points to hybrid BM25/vector search) and design index.md as generated navigation, not the sole retrieval mechanism.
 > Inference: add lint jobs that mirror Glukhov's drift types (source, citation, decision, terminology, structure) as reports, not auto-rewrites, and track health metrics such as pages without sources, pages past review date, and pages changed without human review. Karpathy's lint (contradictions, stale claims, orphans, gaps) is the minimum, not the whole set.
 > Inference: raw documents (regulations, meeting minutes, partner files) are untrusted input to the agent, so plan prompt-injection defenses (secure-llm-wiki pattern: sanitize, independent second-model review of writes, per-claim provenance, revertible commits). Enforce page-level visibility in the API, not by trusting the agent, since neither the gist nor most implementations address access control (Cortex lists it as future work).
 > Inference: staff will author pages too, whereas Karpathy rarely edits by hand. Record page provenance state (human-authored, agent-drafted and human-approved, agent-proposed) since commenters found a type field distinguishing human from AI text useful, and pradocabrero grades trust by extraction provenance. Also name a human owner for each page and a successor process, given the mortality point.
 > Inference: keep Karpathy's exact terms (raw sources, wiki, schema, ingest, query, lint, index, log, file back) as the shared English technical vocabulary and note the project rule against coining Vietnamese compound terms for technical names. Avoid epistemic drift as an unqualified label in product docs, since it is blog-level vocabulary; describe the concrete risk (a misread source persisting into later answers) in plain words.
