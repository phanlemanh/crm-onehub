====================================================================================================
Migration, adoption/change management, review notifications, and export/offline for an owner-approved AI knowledge base (Vietnam context)
--- SUMMARY
Coverage caveat first: this session's WebSearch quota was already spent (200/200), so I worked from direct reads of primary documents, vendor sitemaps and scholarly indexes (OpenAlex/CrossRef); Gartner, APQC, ScienceDirect and Vietnamese law portals were unreachable, so independent evidence is thinner than requested. What I could verify: (1) On migration, the KCS v6 guide (Consortium for Service Innovation) reports that mass legacy migration has never worked for its members and that 90-95% of an old KB is never referenced (member experience, not a measured study); the only quantitative analogue for 'share worth keeping' is the UK Cabinet Office records-review record (about 15%+ valuable among retain-type files, 0-1% among delete-type files, checked by 10%/1,000-file dip sampling). (2) There is no independent failure-rate evidence for self-built company KBs: the 'most die' numbers are Slite's own, and Storey and Barnett only say a large proportion of KM initiatives fail; the best independent evidence on approval gates is Wikimedia's own record that broad flagged-revision review produced backlogs averaging 1000+ days on some wikis, while English Wikipedia keeps its gate on under 0.05% of pages. (3) Reviewer capacity has no direct policy-document data; the only quantified analogues are code review (Google: median 4 reviews per developer per week, 80% under 10, mean 3.2 h/week; Cisco/SmartBear: sessions under about 60 minutes) plus approval-fatigue signals (Anthropic: users accept 93% of permission prompts; Green's review of 41 human-oversight policies). (4) Microsoft's Nudge is the best-documented reminder system (60% faster overdue PRs, at most one notification per PR, 73% positive), and GitHub scheduled reminders, Gerrit's attention set and GitBook's fallback-reviewer rule give reusable patterns; all are code-review analogues. (5) Zalo reaches 78.3M monthly users but its official business-message service (ZBS) is restrictive: pre-approved templates (2-3 working days), officially transaction-based recipients, one message per status change, single language, no links in body, complaint-based suspension; an internal-staff carve-out exists in the template rules but sits uneasily with the policy wording, and two Zalo pages show different prices. (6) On export/offline, vendors offer Markdown/HTML/PDF export but none documents an archival, versioned, approver-stamped PDF; Confluence exports omit version history; Safari evicts offline data after 7 days; so OneHub must build its own snapshots and offline pack. (7) Evidence on Vietnamese organisational factors is sparse: one survey (n=763 employees) on face-concern and a few small studies; I found no direct evidence on reluctance to flag errors.
--- TENSIONS
 * Prune versus keep: KCS says archiving or shrinking a KB treats the symptom and old articles can be the most valuable, while Slite/Intercom/Zendesk and Google advise retiring duplicates, outdated and conflicting content, and AI-specific vendor data claims stale docs cause wrong answers. Likely resolution by content type (inference): troubleshooting knowledge tolerates age, time-bound policies and regulations do not.
 * Demand-driven versus planned: KCS says do not create articles ahead of demand, while a top-20 SOP pilot is planned; KCS itself allows seeding with each trainee's top ten issues, which reconciles the two only partly.
 * Gate everything versus gate a little: the design requires owner approval of every change, but Wikimedia shows full gating creating backlogs (German Wikipedia) while English Wikipedia works by gating under 0.05% of pages; no independent study says which scope suits a company KB.
 * Bulk verification versus honest review: Zendesk (30 at a time), Slite and Guru offer bulk verify to cope with backlogs, while approval-fatigue evidence (93% acceptance, Green's review) argues bulk approval erodes the purpose of review.
 * Verification cadence has no independent optimum: Guru defaults to monthly but recommends at least yearly, Zendesk examples use quarterly and its freshness metric six months; Guru claims 1-2 minutes per card while code-review data suggest tens of minutes per non-trivial change.
 * Notification style: Iqbal and Horvitz found people keep disruptive notifications for awareness and some check more when they are off; Kushlev found alerts raise inattention; Li found users prefer suppression to deferral; Nudge succeeded with a single targeted message. No source settles digest versus immediate for document review.
 * Incentives: KCS rejects activity goals and cash rewards but also says knowledge work should ultimately be part of job descriptions and formal reviews; an OKR link is safe only if it measures outcomes.
 * Automation versus human judgement in triage: Cabinet Office reports its lexicon beats human reviewers (99.4% versus about 1% human error) yet keeps deletion a human decision; Green argues human oversight of algorithms often fails; MarkItDown and GitBook use AI for conversion and cleanup while KCS distrusts mass migration.
 * Zalo policy: the policy page defines recipients as prior-transaction customers and allows one message per status change, but the template rules mention internal staff notices and the audit-proof list includes employees and collaborators; and two Zalo pages show different per-message prices.
 * Bystander effect design: Guru group verifiers appear in every member's queue until someone acts, while Gerrit's attention set and Nudge target a specific blocker; no study compares them.
--- IMPLICATIONS
 > (a) PHASED 0-90 DAY PLAN. Scope note: KCS says adoption waves run 12 weeks to a year, so 90 days covers planning plus the first wave, not full rollout. DAYS 0-14 'Set the rails' (KCS Plan and Design; Slite): pick ONE pilot department and one wave of 25-50 staff; name a part-time KB lead/coach (KCS: coaches about half-time) plus one owner and one deputy per pilot page; adopt a content standard (Intercom 14 factors / Zendesk guidance) and a reviewer budget (see c); take baselines (legacy file count, share with a named owner, staff questions per week, approval latency). URGENT: harvest Zalo-group files now (Zalo keeps files on servers only briefly and its backup excludes files/videos) and store originals unchanged with provenance. In parallel, decide the notification channel: if ZBS is wanted, buy OA Growth plan (2.5M VND/yr), file templates (2-3 working days) and get written Zalo confirmation that internal staff reminders are in scope; otherwise default to in-app plus email. DAYS 15-45 'Pilot on the top ~20 documents' (KCS top-ten seeding; Intercom/Slite/Guru high-traffic-first; the number 20 is an inference): choose by demand (repeated staff questions, weekly-use files, audit relevance); import files as immutable sources; agent drafts pages; owner approves each diff; Cabinet-Office-style dip sampling (10% or 1,000 items) to estimate triage error; one canonical page per topic, conflicts resolved by owners before publish (Google SWE); run the company agent from approved pages only, in shadow mode first (inference); hold a fixed weekly review slot. Track time to first decision, approval latency, edit-before-approve rate, and share of staff questions answered with a citation. DAYS 46-75 'Second wave and demand-driven migration' (KCS waves: about 35-40, then about 100, then the rest): add 2-3 departments; make old folders read-only with banners (Slite) and search KB first, legacy second; every legacy fetch that is not in the KB becomes a migration request (KCS demand rule); switch on the escalation ladder in (d); recognise owners publicly, no cash and no article-count targets (KCS). DAYS 76-90 'Prove and decide': run an export/restore drill and a PDF-snapshot audit sample, test the offline pack on staff phones, review Wikimedia-style KPIs (share of pages with expired verification, median review time, oldest pending item), start a bi-weekly KB council (KCS), then gate expansion on capacity rather than calendar.
 > (b) MIGRATION TRIAGE RUBRIC (decision rules; sources in brackets, rule thresholds are inference unless noted). Step 1 Inventory: record file, path, format, owner guess, last modified, last opened, duplicate group; dedupe by hash and near-duplicate title (Cabinet Office classifies on dates, format and duplicates). Step 2 Hard gates: contains personal data or customer records -> route to data-protection check under PDPL before any AI ingestion; carries a statutory or contractual retention duty -> ARCHIVE, never delete (Slite 'archive' class). Step 3 Authority: pick the latest effective, signed/decision-numbered version; older versions -> ARCHIVE as superseded (Google canonical-document rule). Step 4 Owner: no person accepts ownership within the triage window -> keep out of the agent's corpus (ARCHIVE unowned) (Google SWE: ownerless docs rot). Step 5 Demand and currency: opened or asked about in last 12 months, or cited by an audit -> MIGRATE-NOW candidate; not used for 12+ months and not statutory -> ARCHIVE or RETIRE (Slite one-year heuristic; KCS 90-95% never referenced); used occasionally -> MIGRATE-ON-DEMAND with legacy read-only (KCS). Step 6 Conflict: two live documents disagree -> owner picks the canonical one before ingest (Slite: one owning team per topic). Step 7 Format handling: Word - ingest text, keep original because converters drop comments/tracked changes (Notion); PDF - check text layer vs scan, OCR needed for scans, verify tables by eye (Docling/MarkItDown/Notion caveats; Vietnamese diacritic accuracy untested); Excel - treat as data, not prose: keep as attachment and only write reviewed rules/thresholds into pages (inference); Google Docs - export docx/pdf and record share permissions; Zalo files and threads - files harvested as sources; chat statements are hints, not policy, until an owner confirms (inference). Step 8 AI readiness: apply the Intercom 14 factors / Zendesk guidance; prefer focused pages of about 1,000 words or less (Guru claim, vendor); avoid tables where possible (Zendesk). Outputs: MIGRATE-NOW, MIGRATE-ON-DEMAND, ARCHIVE (retained, not visible to the agent), RETIRE (delete after retention check, human decision). Expectation for planning: roughly 5-15% of legacy documents end up migrated (inference from KCS 5-10% ever referenced and Cabinet Office about 15% valuable in retain-type files); no independent figure exists for policy/SOP libraries. Recommended stance: import as immutable sources and let the agent draft pages for owner approval, not bulk-convert (KCS never-worked report; converter losses; agent-drafted change requests are the pattern GitBook, Zendesk copilot and Intercom Operator all use).
 > (c) OWNER ASSIGNMENT AND REVIEW LOAD. Direct evidence for policy/SOP diffs per owner per week: NONE FOUND. Analogues: Google reviewers median 4 reviews/week, 80% under 10, mean 3.2 h/week (code review is core work for them); Cisco: keep sessions under about 60 minutes; Wikimedia: gates wider than reviewer capacity produce months-to-years backlogs; KCS: coaches 1:5-1:8 at start, about 1:50 mature, and a goal of zero articles waiting in the publish queue; Zendesk/Slite/Guru all provide bulk verify, which is exactly where approval fatigue arises (Anthropic 93% acceptance). Provisional defaults (INFERENCE, tune from pilot data): cap open proposals at about 5 per owner per week in the pilot and never above 10 (anchored to Google's median and 80th percentile); budget about 10-20 minutes per proposal for a page of up to about 1,000 words (reading alone is about 4 minutes at 238 wpm; checking against the source is my estimate at 2.5-5x); total about 2 hours a week per owner or less because policy owners have other jobs; sittings of 45-60 minutes; rate-limit the agent so it cannot flood one owner; separate low-risk edits (typos, links, formatting) into a weekly batch with a single approve and require individual review for changes to numbers, thresholds, dates, legal references. Keep approvals honest: show the source excerpt beside each change, log dwell time, and alert if approvals approach 95% with near-zero dwell time; have a second person spot-check 20-33% of approved changes each quarter (Cisco spot-check finding). Re-verification load: pages per owner x 12 / interval months; monthly cadence (Guru default) is not sustainable at scale, so use risk tiers (inference): regulation/policy pages every 6 months or on a law-change trigger, operational SOPs every 12 months, low-risk pages every 12-24 months (Guru itself advises at least yearly; Zendesk freshness uses six months). Owner design: one owner plus one deputy; the deputy is notified only on escalation, not in parallel (Guru group-queue design shows everyone sees the task until someone acts - a diffusion risk, inference); GitBook-style fallback so an unowned or unresponsive item goes to KB admins; reassign ownership when someone changes role (Zendesk warns downgraded owners keep receiving emails). Owner recognition: public credit and role status, not cash; tie any OKR/KPI to outcomes (citation-answer accuracy, time-to-decision, expired-page share) not counts of pages or approvals (KCS: activity goals corrupt the KB).
 > (d) DEFAULT NOTIFICATION POLICY AND CHANNELS. Principle: one system of record, few interrupts, target the person who is actually blocking (Nudge, Gerrit). Default ladder (INFERENCE built from those patterns): T0 proposal created -> in-app 'Your turn' queue with waiting time (Gerrit) plus a single message to the owner only; low-risk changes go into the next digest instead of an immediate ping. Daily personal digest at a fixed time, at most one; weekly team digest with minimum-age and minimum-staleness filters (GitHub scheduled reminders). T+2 working days still pending -> one reminder to the owner stating what is blocked (Nudge sends at most one per item; 73% positive). T+5 -> notify the deputy. T+10 -> KB admin and line manager; mark the item 'review overdue' on a dashboard (Wikimedia suggests tracking median review time and oldest unreviewed age). Expiry: notify owner 30 days and 7 days before, then on the day; on expiry show 'verification expired' and let policy choose between banner-only and exclusion from the agent (Slite excludes outdated docs from its agent; Notion notifies on lapse). Suppress notifications for agent drafts that fail automated checks; allow per-person quiet hours and a daily cap (interruptions raise stress and inattention; users prefer suppression to deferral). CHANNEL OPTIONS FOR VIETNAM: (1) In-app queue plus email as the always-on baseline (Notion, Zendesk and Guru all rely on email). (2) Zalo ZBS template messages via API: reach is excellent (78.3M MAU) but constraints are real - needs verified OA on Growth (2.5M VND/yr incl. VAT) or Comprehensive plan, templates approved in 2-3 working days, Vietnamese-only or English-only text, no links in body (CTA button instead), parameters with identifiers, 'Thong bao noi bo' marker for staff notices, per-message cost about 200-300 VND (or 300-400 per the policy page) plus VAT, proof of employment on audit, suspension above 5% complaints or 50 in 24 h, escalating to account lock; the policy's transaction-only wording versus the staff carve-out must be confirmed in writing with Zalo. Reminder text should reuse one approved template with parameters (page code, owner name, days waiting) - my inference. Rough cost for 30 owners x 5 notices a week is about 1.6-3.1M VND a year in messages (arithmetic on the quoted rates), so risk, not cost, is the issue. (3) Zalo OA advisory messages are free inside the 48-hour window after the user last interacts and 55 VND outside it (allowance included in plan); a staff opt-in to the OA would make this usable (inference). (4) Zalo Bot Platform Basic is free but limited to 50 users per bot and 3,000 messages a month; Pro was not yet available. (5) OA-owned Zalo groups for team digests: 25,000-300,000 VND a month for 10-1,000 members; OA-to-group messages free until 31 Dec 2026; group posts risk noise and diffusion. (6) Microsoft Teams via Workflows webhooks (connectors retired May 2026; fixed bot identity; Adaptive Cards for buttons). (7) Google Chat webhooks (1 request per second per space). (8) Slack and Lark: not verified in this pass. Before choosing, survey staff on which channel they actually read at work (no independent data found on enterprise messaging share in Vietnam).
 > (e) EXPORT AND OFFLINE REQUIREMENTS. 1) Canonical store in your own database; every approved version immutable with version id, approver, approval time, source document hashes and diff to previous. 2) Per-page and bulk export as Markdown with YAML frontmatter, aligned to Open Knowledge Format v0.2 fields (type, sources, generated, verified with a human actor, status, stale_after) so the corpus is portable to git or any tool; include attachments and original source files. 3) Nightly full export to storage you control plus a tested restore drill (Notion/Confluence/GitBook export only what the exporter can see, and Confluence omits history). 4) Versioned PDF snapshot on demand and on each approval: header/footer with page title, version, effective date, owner, approver, approval timestamp, document hash and a note that printed copies are uncontrolled; generated server-side rather than via browser print (GitBook's PDF is a print flow and none of the vendors documents stamped snapshots). 5) Audit view by date: reproduce exactly what was published on a past date, with its audit trail, using 21 CFR 11.10 as a checklist (human-readable copies, retrievability for the retention period, time-stamped trails that do not obscure earlier data, access controls). 6) Retention: keep every published version for at least the longest statutory or contractual period that applies; Vietnamese retention and e-document rules were NOT verified and need legal input; apply PDPL to personal data in sources. 7) Printable SOPs: print stylesheet, one page per SOP where possible, QR code back to the live page and version check. 8) Mobile-first read view: fast on phones (Vietnam has 137M mobile connections; Zalo is the daily app), Vietnamese-first UI. 9) Offline: an installable web app with a small offline pack of pinned SOPs (approved version only, PDF fallback), visible 'last synced' and 'may be outdated' labels, re-sync on open; Safari evicts offline data after 7 days for non-installed sites and Confluence's offline mode is similarly limited, so require Add to Home Screen for field staff and test on iOS. 10) Zalo-friendly sharing: share a link or PDF into Zalo groups with the version stamp so chat copies are traceable. 11) No dependence on a vendor's proprietary format; exports must include the agent citation map (which pages an answer used) for audit.
 > (f) TOP 10 PITFALLS with sources and severity. 1) Approval gate wider than reviewer capacity -> backlogs (Wikimedia: 1000+ day average age on some wikis; English Wikipedia gates under 0.05% of pages; Google load figures) - CRITICAL. 2) Rubber-stamp approval from fatigue or bulk-verify (Anthropic 93% acceptance; Green; Zendesk/Guru/Slite bulk verify) - HIGH. 3) Bulk-converting legacy content (KCS: never worked; Notion/GitBook/HubSpot import losses; MarkItDown fidelity note) - HIGH. 4) Ownerless or churned owners (Google SWE; Zendesk downgraded-owner emails; Slite vendor data) - HIGH. 5) Big-bang rollout beyond about 50 people and no coaching or leadership change (KCS: management failure is the most frequent failure point; 6-8 weeks of relaxed expectations) - HIGH. 6) Activity-based incentives such as page or approval counts (KCS 7.3/8.6) - MEDIUM-HIGH. 7) Notification overload or diffusion of responsibility (Nudge one-per-item design; Kushlev N=221; Iqbal and Horvitz; Guru group queues) - MEDIUM-HIGH. 8) Zalo policy and template constraints and account-lock enforcement; internal-staff use ambiguous; conflicting price pages (Zalo terms/pricing) - HIGH for the channel, MEDIUM overall. 9) Losing Zalo-shared files before harvest (Zalo help: short server retention, no file backup) - HIGH but one-time and preventable now. 10) Stale content answered confidently by AI (Slite survey, vendor: 76% saw a wrong answer from a stale doc) and answering from unapproved sources (HubSpot private sources can surface in answers) - HIGH if unmitigated; mitigate with expiry-aware retrieval and approved-only corpus. Also watch: Safari 7-day offline eviction (WebKit/Atlassian) - MEDIUM; personal data in migrated files under PDPL from 1 Jan 2026 - MEDIUM, unverified detail.
 > (g) VENDOR-ONLY OR SELF-REPORTED CLAIMS (treat as unverified by independent parties). Slite: 94% of content untouched monthly; 76% of users never create a doc; top 1% write 47%; 76% of surveyed had an AI tool surface a stale doc with a wrong answer and the follow-on percentages (58%, 41%, 40%, 35%, 29%, 22%); more than 90% of 200-plus audited articles outdated; 2-3 week Confluence Cloud migration; 'verified docs rank higher' effect. Guru: verifying a card takes 1-2 minutes; users do best with content under 1,000 words; recommends at least yearly review; and secondary statistics on its governance page (29% cannot extract knowledge, 28% doubt accuracy, 21% of time searching plus 14% recreating, 9% ready, 47% higher success, 23% productivity lift) which it attributes to third parties I did not check. Intercom: Operator cuts a 2-3 hour multi-article update to under 10 minutes; a medium change touches 8-10 articles; 14-factor framework effectiveness (no published validation). Zendesk: knowledge copilot metrics and recommendations (early access), generative-AI content practices (no published effect sizes). HubSpot and Notion: no outcome claims verified. SmartBear: Cisco-study figures (200-400 lines, 500 lines/hour, 60 minutes, 20-33% spot-check) come from a vendor-run study. Anthropic: 93% acceptance is first-party telemetry. KCS: 90-95% of old KB never referenced, coach ratios, 12-week to 1-year adoption, benefits proportional to coaching - consortium member experience, not independent measurement. Cabinet Office: accuracy figures (99.4%, human error about 1%) are the department's own pilot. Zalo pricing and policy pages are first-party and inconsistent with each other.
 > OTHER IMPLICATIONS. Keep approval scope tiered and small at launch (only pilot department pages need owner approval), and grow scope only as measured review latency stays healthy. Store legacy files as immutable sources even when they are archived, so any page can be traced to a source. Use Vietnamese-language, single-language notification templates (Zalo forbids mixing languages in a template; the KB itself may mix Vietnamese and English terms, but I found no evidence on retrieval effects). Do not let chat-group statements become policy without owner confirmation. Confirm channel rules with Zalo in writing before designing around ZBS.
