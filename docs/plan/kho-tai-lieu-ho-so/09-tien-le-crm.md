====================================================================================================
CRM and support-platform knowledge base precedents (Intercom, Zendesk, Freshdesk, HubSpot, Salesforce, Atlassian, Notion, Guru, Slite, Glean) and how AI is being layered on, 2025-2026
--- SUMMARY
Across ten mature products, four patterns converge. First, lifecycle: Draft, In review, Approved or Ready to publish, Published, Archived is standard on paid tiers (Zendesk Team Publishing, Freshdesk, HubSpot, Confluence Premium), and the published version stays live while a revision waits for review. Second, freshness: named owner plus expiring verification is the norm in knowledge-first tools (Zendesk Enterprise, Notion, Guru, Slite, Glean), but only Slite and Guru explicitly tie verification status to what the AI may use; I found no verification or review-date feature in Intercom, HubSpot or Freshdesk docs. Third, in 2026 the AI layer shifted from answering to proposing edits: Intercom Fin Operator (GA 6 Aug 2026), Slite Agent (10 Jun 2026), HubSpot Knowledge Base Agent, Jira Service Management AI drafts, and Guru Knowledge Agents (Jan 2026). Nearly all route changes through a human-approved proposal or diff, which is the same shape as the OneHub design; Guru is the outlier, moving toward AI auto-verification. Fourth, no vendor documents a hard guarantee that the AI answers only from the knowledge base. They combine retrieval grounding, confidence thresholds, not-understood fallbacks, source allowlists or scopes, human handoff and optional citations, and Glean's own docs say answers can be produced without any retrieval. Analytics converge on no-result searches, helpful votes, per-article usage versus resolution, and AI-generated gap suggestions with explicit thresholds. Internal SOP and policy libraries are second-class in helpdesk products (no drafts, restricted content excluded from AI), although Zendesk launched an Employee Service AI agent EAP in Jul-Aug 2026. Independent evidence on regrets is thin: most of it is vendor-authored (Slite's 149-respondent survey says maintenance kills most self-built company brains), plus the Air Canada chatbot ruling as a liability precedent.
--- TENSIONS
 * Human verification versus AI auto-verification: Guru (Jan 2026) has agents verify and unverify content from natural-language rules and hide unverified results, citing that manual review reaches only 8-12 percent of content; Slite, Intercom Operator and HubSpot's Knowledge Base Agent keep a human approving every change, and Slite's survey says 78 percent trust a named human owner most. Guru still routes critical content to experts.
 * Native versus connected knowledge: Intercom advises maintaining content natively because external content refreshes weekly and synced content is read-only, while Zendesk (about 24 hours for external sources), Freshdesk (about 15 minutes for SharePoint), Guru and Glean sell connectors over existing systems with inherited permissions.
 * Knowledge-base-only answers versus product reality: Freshdesk calls the KB the primary source of truth, HubSpot and Zendesk gate with confidence and not-understood fallbacks, Notion searches the web by default, and Glean documents pre-trained answers with no retrieval. Nobody documents a hard guarantee; hallucination claims are vendor-authored (Intercom avoids hallucinations; Clientell 3-27 percent).
 * Citations optional versus mandatory: HubSpot does not cite private sources yet may still use them; Freshdesk lets admins hide references per source; Intercom cites synced GitHub content only if public_url is set; Notion Enterprise Search always cites; Glean cites except when no retrieval occurs; Guru cites to the section.
 * Verification meaning: Zendesk and Notion verification are expiry-driven owner reminders whose docs do not say the AI is affected; Slite and Guru make verification change ranking, exclusion or visibility; Intercom, HubSpot and Freshdesk docs I read have no verification concept at all.
 * Permission model: Notion AI and Glean inherit the asker's permissions, while Notion Custom Agents use their own allow-list and can reveal more than the asker could open; Freshdesk excludes restricted articles from AI entirely; Zendesk uses restricted articles only for authenticated customers; HubSpot warns private content may surface uncited.
 * Effort claims disagree: Intercom says Operator cuts a release's content update to under an hour, yet its own table lists about 30 hours a week for product-update content; Slite says maintenance is what kills company brains, while Guru says AI can take over the verification workload.
 * Learning from feedback: Intercom says Suggestions learn from rejected suggestions, while Glean says thumbs-down feedback goes to human review and does not retrain automatically.
 * Internal versus customer-facing depth: JSM internal KBs lack drafts and categories and Intercom internal articles have no draft state, yet Atlassian calls internal KBs the foundation of future agent AI and Zendesk positions employee agents as a distinct product because customer-facing tools fall short.
--- IMPLICATIONS
 > Model page state on three separate axes, as KCS v6 does (inference): lifecycle (Draft, In review, Published, Archived), audience (which roles or teams may see and ask about the page), and confidence (verified until date, owner). Keep the published version live while a revision awaits review, as Freshdesk, Zendesk and Confluence do, so approvals are always diffs against the live page.
 > Copy the proposal object from Fin Operator and the Slite Agent: agent output is a diff-bearing proposal with a change message, typed actions (edit, create, rename, archive, change owner), a visible reasoning trace, and links to the source passages that motivated it. The agent has no write path to Published. Add what neither documents: show the exact source sentence next to each changed line.
 > Give approvers the controls HubSpot and Salesforce practice forced on them: due date, request changes, all-versus-one approver threshold for high-risk pages, reassign and admin recall for absent approvers, and an audit record of any bypass. Avoid HubSpot's Super Admin skip path for regulated pages.
 > Implement verified-until with a named owner (Zendesk, Notion, Guru, Slite pattern) and decide what the employee agent does on expiry. Slite excludes expired or outdated docs from its agent; for a finance marketplace, expired regulatory or policy pages should be excluded or answered with an explicit verification-expired caveat, chosen by page risk class. Do not allow indefinite verification on regulated pages (Notion allows it). Handle owner departure explicitly, since Zendesk leaves such articles stuck.
 > Build the gap loop on the same review pipeline: log every unanswered, low-confidence or downvoted agent question, cluster it, and route it to the owning page as a proposal (Intercom recommendations with impact score and 4-week expiry; Slite Ask Insights assign, re-test, solve; JSM threshold of about 5 related requests in 30 days). Track per-page cited-count versus helpful or escalated outcome, the Intercom involved versus resolved view, not just page views.
 > Run a weekly contradiction and duplicate lint across the wiki (Intercom does about 15 contradictions plus 5 duplicates a week). Contradictory pages are the direct cause in Moffatt v. Air Canada; a cross-linked wiki reduces duplication but does not remove it.
 > Make citations mandatory and structural for the policy library: page title, section anchor, version and verified date, linking to the approved page. Several products let admins hide citations (HubSpot private sources, Freshdesk hide references, Intercom without public_url); for internal policy answers the citation is the audit trail. Guru and Glean deep-link to the exact passage, which is the bar to copy.
 > Enforce KB-only answering server-side rather than by prompt: no vendor documents a hard guarantee, and Glean explicitly allows pre-trained answers without retrieval. Expose only approved-wiki retrieval to the agent, turn off web search, refuse or hand off when no page clears a relevance threshold (Zendesk Not understood, HubSpot three-way confidence), and auto-hand-off high-risk financial or legal advice as Fin does. Log refusals as gap signals.
 > Apply page-level audience at retrieval time using the asking employee's identity (the Notion AI, Glean, Rovo, Guru model). Avoid an agent-wide allow-list without the asker's permissions, which Notion documents as able to expose more than the asker can see. Default-deny new pages until approved and audience-set, as Intercom does for internal articles and GitHub-synced content.
 > Encode authoring standards once and apply them twice, as the drafting agent's instructions and as a pre-review lint shown to the human reviewer. Use the convergent rules from Intercom's 14 factors, Freshdesk, and Atlassian: one topic per page, self-contained sections, question-style headings, exact numbers, defined terms, stated audience, documented limitations. Store the style guide as a versioned wiki page, the equivalent of Operator memory.
 > Keep a golden question set and re-run it against proposed changes before approval (Rovo evaluation CSV up to 100 prompts, Intercom Operator testing against historical conversations, HubSpot batch testing, Glean golden sets). Show reviewers which agent answers would change. Version the agent's own configuration with promote and rollback, as Zendesk and Glean now do.
 > Plan reviewer capacity as a first-class constraint: Intercom's own team budgets about 1 hour weekly for AI suggestions, 5 hours for human flags and a large weekly product-update effort, and Slite's survey attributes most abandonment to upkeep. Provide a triage view, bulk actions (Zendesk caps bulk verify at 30), prioritisation by usage (Guru relevancy score, Intercom impact score) and auto-archive of unused, unverified pages (Guru).
 > Decide the source of truth deliberately if markdown in git is used. Intercom's GitHub sync shows the frontmatter fields worth mirroring (title, description, audience, state, per-agent enable flags, public URL for citation) and warns of costs: one-way sync, read-only in product, non-blocking checks. Intercom also recommends native editing because external refresh is slow.
 > Treat source documents and minutes as untrusted input to the ingest agent (Notion's prompt-injection guidance): restrict its tools to producing proposals, keep the human diff as the safety control, exclude sensitive classifications from drafting inputs (JSM excludes security-level items), and mask personal data before sending text to the model provider (Zendesk's sanitisation pattern).
 > Capture feedback at the point of use: a Report this answer or Flag this paragraph action that creates a task assigned to the page owner, as Zendesk Knowledge Capture (auto-assigned to owner on Enterprise) and Freshdesk (No vote becomes a ticket with the author as watcher) do. Intercom's Improve answer button, jumping from an answer to its source page, is the UX target.
