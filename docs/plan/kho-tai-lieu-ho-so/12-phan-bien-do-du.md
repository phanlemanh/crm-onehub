CRITIQUE
Coverage of the LLM Wiki pattern itself is good. The pattern and ecosystem are mapped, the failure modes are catalogued with real bug reports, the review-gate precedents (Intercom, Slite, Vouch, atomicstrata, Meta) are documented, and the navigation, retrieval and stack choices have concrete data. What a team building this end to end would still NOT know falls into six areas:

1. Legal and compliance for a Vietnamese company. The only sources are law-firm and news summaries that disagree with each other. Provider and region analysis assumes Anthropic first-party or Bedrock. Per the repo owner's notes, the existing agent calls models through a Vercel AI Gateway key, and no agent examined that path.
2. Vietnamese-language quality. Every agent flagged it and none measured it on real documents: scans and OCR, legacy fonts, embeddings, tokenizer cost, and drafting fidelity. The one legal-reasoning benchmark used (VietLegal) tested 2024-25 models.
3. Legal-document validity metadata. There is no researched source of truth for hiệu lực, amendment chains or consolidated versions, yet this is what makes regulation pages go stale.
4. The concrete data model and permission model. The results give principles but no schema-level precedent. The visibility of a page derived from several sources is called unsettled, and that was never checked against Microsoft Purview label inheritance or Glean.
5. Evaluation protocol and success metrics. There are no defensible targets, golden-set sizing, adoption or health benchmarks, or go/no-go criteria for a pilot.
6. Migration of existing documents, change management, notifications, and export/offline needs. There is essentially nothing on these.

Evidence quality is uneven. The strongest quantitative claims are single-author preprints with LLM judges, vendor blogs, or self-reports. Most of the human-in-the-loop evidence is by analogy from code review, clinical decision support and agent permission prompts. No study tests reviewers approving LLM-drafted wiki or policy diffs, which is the central assumption of the OneHub design.

Two items are internal and not researchable on the web:
- The corpus inventory: how many documents, in which formats, what share is scanned, and how many staff or roles per permission tier.
- Reviewer capacity: how many owners exist and how many hours a week they can spend on review.

Sizing, cost and pilot criteria depend on these, so they should be collected before or alongside the research.

One design tension needs the user to decide, not a researcher. The stated requirement is that an owner approves every AI-proposed change. Several agents recommend auto-applying purely mechanical fixes (typos, links) with an audit log, which would relax that requirement.
--- WEAK CLAIMS
 - Wiki beats RAG on cross-document synthesis and on claim-level citation support (40.2% vs 18.9%). This rests on one single-author preprint (Cochran): 24 papers, 13 questions, LLM judges with near-zero agreement on groundedness (rho 0.04). It scores alignment with the wiki's own excerpt, not fidelity to the original PDF, and its structural-integrity advantage missed the preregistered bar. Several agents call it the best controlled evidence. Treat it as suggestive only.
 - Cost claims contradict each other. The same preprint measured about 21x more query tokens for the wiki agent (max reasoning effort, 30-turn cap). Another paper (ISC) reports 2.2K vs 16.3K reader tokens in favour of compiled claims, and Meta reports about 80% fewer tokens per turn. Corpus2Skill reports 7-13x more per query. The architectures and corpora differ, so none transfers to a Vietnamese policy corpus. No measured cost exists for wiki ingest; the 35K-token and $5-50 per month figures are one blogger's back-of-envelope.
 - Scale ceilings (about 100 sources, 150-200 pages, 500, 1,000, 10,000) come from mixed sources. Only Karpathy's roughly 100 articles / 400K words is first-hand. The rest are blog estimates, single-user self-reports or unsupported comments. The 709-page result comes from one corpus and one model with a failed judge audit (kappa 0.23). Several ecosystem and risk findings blame whole-file rewrites and flat indexes rather than page count. No number applies to OneHub.
 - Vendor or self-promotional material carries heavy weight in places: Falconer, Mem0, Neo4j, Atlan, Epsilla, Penfield, Sanity, innobu (consultancy), agentmemory and LLM Wiki v2 (95.2% R@5), Intercom (97% automation, effort figures), Guru (manual review reaches only 8-12% of content), Clientell (3-27% hallucination), LlamaIndex, and qmd, GD4AI and nashsu self-benchmarks. The Slite survey is n=149, self-selected and vendor-run (55% gave up). LangChain's OpenWiki eval (92.9% to 97.8% supported) is vendor-run and on code. GD4AI's comparison table mislabels a competitor.
 - Human-approval evidence is by analogy only. The Anthropic figures (93% vs 97% of permission prompts approved, 13.6% catch rate, about 5% after 50+ prompts) are vendor-run, about shell commands, and the two approval rates are unreconciled. Radiology, pathology and code-review studies are not wiki review. No study covers owners approving LLM-drafted policy diffs, so 'owner approval is the main control' is an untested assumption. The proposed mitigations (seeded errors, forced first judgment, claim-level cards) have no wiki-specific validation.
 - Open Knowledge Format (OKF) v0.2 is presented as an emerging standard, but the spec is weeks old and a v0.3 is proposed. It has no owner or supersession field, and adoption beyond Google samples and OpenWiki is unproven. Agents cite two different canonical locations (GoogleCloudPlatform/knowledge-catalog okf/SPEC.md vs GoogleCloudPlatform/open-knowledge-format). Verify before copying field names.
 - Legal and residency findings rest on law-firm and news summaries that disagree. One reports a dossier due within 60 days of the transfer, EY describes a 45-day process, and they differ on whether overseas cloud is explicitly covered. The draft Data Security Law is not enacted. It is unresolved whether the AI Law's finance-sector transition applies to this company, and the Air Canada ruling was read only through Wikipedia. The residency, retention and Covered-Model analysis assumes Anthropic first-party or Bedrock. The existing agent appears to use a Vercel AI Gateway key, which no agent examined. Provider, region and zero-data-retention conclusions may not carry over.
 - Vietnamese-specific findings are thin or indirect. 'No project documents Vietnamese support' and 'no Vietnamese-specific editor issue found' are absence claims made after the search budget ran out, and one agent found an uninspected Vietnamese implementation (mduongvandinh/llm-wiki). VietLegal (16 of 23 models scored 0.00 F1 on conflict detection) tested 2024-25 models, yet it is used to justify design choices. VMMU OCR figures come from exam images with 2025 models, not scanned official documents. VN-MTEB is machine-translated from English and dated mid-2025. The sparse-vs-dense Vietnamese labour-law result is cross-lingual. The inkeep tokenizer fix for Vietnamese was never confirmed.
 - Stack measurements are narrow. The editor round-trip test used one 25-line sample with stock configurations and no custom wikilink or callout extensions. Bundle sizes are esbuild numbers, not Next.js chunks. The extraction tests used one Chrome-generated PDF and two synthetic DOCX files: no real OneHub documents, no scans, no TCVN3/VNI fonts. The graph performance figures come from one Apple Silicon machine. LiteParse benchmark numbers are vendor-run.
 - Navigation claims are soft. 'The global graph is mostly decorative' rests on forum threads, blogs, one CEO's workflow, and no usage telemetry. The matrix-vs-node-link thresholds come from abstracts, and a later study complicates them. Notion having no graph, Confluence having no backlinks and GitBook's features come from snippets, old Jira tickets or partly 404 docs.
 - 'No settled ACL model for LLM-derived pages' and 'no production system implements it' are absence claims. The search budget ran out before checking how Microsoft 365 Copilot (Purview label inheritance), Glean, Google and Notion label or restrict AI-generated content derived from several sources.
 - Positive success stories are weaker evidence than they look: Newton's 1,440 pages, distorx's 7,500 notes, Rakuten-style team wikis, Retrieval-as-Reasoning F1 gains and the Meta second-brain report. They are self-reported or benchmark QA, not correctness of individual policy statements. Newton verifies against originals by hand, which is the cost the pattern promised to remove. Grokipedia and DeepWiki inaccuracy reports are second-hand.
 - Primary-source gaps on the origin story: gist star and fork counts were never confirmed (5,000+ is a snippet). The tweet text came through a mirror. The Sequoia Ascent post is an AI-generated summary Karpathy endorsed, so it is paraphrase. The 'epistemic drift' label is secondary-blog vocabulary, not Karpathy's.
--- GAPS
[Vietnamese law and compliance for an internal AI knowledge base, and the model-provider path (gateway, region, retention)] why: It decides which document classes may be sent to an overseas model, whether a cross-border transfer dossier is needed, what records and audit logs must be kept, and which provider or region can be used. The earlier research relied on law-firm summaries that contradict each other on the dossier timeline and on overseas cloud. It also analysed only Anthropic first-party and Bedrock, not the gateway route the existing agent appears to use. A wrong assumption here can block launch or create liability.
[Vietnamese-language document ingestion, retrieval and drafting quality: scans and OCR, legacy encodings, embeddings, segmentation, tokenizer cost] why: Every agent flagged Vietnamese as unresearched. Existing evidence is indirect: exam-image OCR (CER from 4% to over 200%), a machine-translated embedding benchmark, a cross-lingual legal test, and 2024-25 models. Without a tested pipeline, source extraction, search, citation quote checks and diff review can silently fail on diacritics or fonts, and the approval loop would rest on wrong text.
[Authoritative sources and data model for Vietnamese legal-document validity (hieu luc), amendment chains and consolidated versions] why: Regulation pages go stale when a decree is amended, replaced or partly repealed. The earlier research concluded that LLMs are poor at conflict and amendment detection and that structured validity data is needed, but nobody found where authoritative status data lives, whether it can be reused legally, or how to model article-level validity. This decides the page schema, stale detection and the reviewer's conflict card.
[Data model, lifecycle and permission model for an approval-gated, AI-drafted wiki on Postgres: production precedents and ACL inheritance for derived pages] why: The results give principles (approved-revision pointer, base-revision precondition, source hashes, claim ledger) but no schema-level precedent. Agents also found no settled rule for who may see a page synthesised from several sources with different visibility, and did not check how Microsoft, Google or Glean handle it. This is the core build spec and the main leak risk. Titles, snippets, backlinks and the graph can all reveal restricted pages.
[Evaluation protocol and success metrics for an owner-approved AI knowledge base: what to measure, how big the test set must be, defensible targets] why: The results say 'build a golden set' and 'measure seeded-error catch rate' but give no protocol, sizes or targets. They give no adoption or health benchmarks and no go/no-go pilot criteria. Without them nobody can tell whether answers are correct, whether the agent refuses when it should, whether restricted pages leak, or whether owners keep up, and the LLM judges used in the literature were shown to be unreliable on exactly the citation-support question.
[Migrating existing documents, change management and adoption, review notifications, and export or offline needs for an owner-approved AI knowledge base] why: The results cover the AI and wiki mechanics but contain essentially nothing on how existing Word, PDF, Excel and chat-shared documents get in, how owners are assigned and kept engaged, how review and expiry reminders should reach people, or what export and offline access auditors and field staff need. Vendor surveys claim most self-built company brains die of upkeep, but no independent evidence was found. Reviewer capacity is the binding constraint, so an unresearched rollout is the likeliest cause of failure.
