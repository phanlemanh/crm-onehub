====================================================================================================
Graph view, backlinks and navigation UX for LLM-Wiki-style knowledge bases (Obsidian, Logseq, Roam, Foam, Quartz, Notion, Confluence, GitBook, Docusaurus, LLM-wiki visualisers), including accessibility and scale
--- SUMMARY
The public evidence says the global node-link graph is mostly a diagnostic, not a navigation tool. Practitioners, Obsidian's own CEO and a curated tier list converge on: global graph = occasional health check (orphans, hubs, clusters); local graph at depth 1-2 = mildly useful; day-to-day navigation runs on quick switcher/command palette, search, inline links and the backlinks panel. Karpathy's April 2026 gist itself frames the graph as the human's lens on wiki shape (hubs, orphans) and relies on index.md plus lint, not the graph, for the agent's navigation. I found no public usage telemetry for graph or backlinks in any PKM tool, so "used vs decoration" rests on forum threads, blogs and creator workflows (small, self-selected samples). The only hard behavioural data on link previews is Wikimedia's Page Previews A/B tests (disable rate around 0.01-0.03%). LLM-wiki visualisers are converging on the same fixes for the untyped hairball: colour by frontmatter (type, confidence, maturity, review status), typed edges that carry the sentence explaining the link, community detection with cohesion scores, "knowledge gap" and orphan lists, and local views; several also feed the same link graph into retrieval. Scale has two very different ceilings: rendering (WebGL/canvas handle thousands of nodes; Obsidian's moderator calls >25K files impractical) versus legibility (a few hundred nodes, sometimes far fewer). Accessibility is the weakest area: node-link canvases are not keyboard or screen-reader operable by default, WAI-ARIA Graphics defines no node-link roles, and the credible pattern is a real list/tree/table as the primary representation with the graph as an enhancement, plus WCAG 2.2 constraints on dragging (2.5.7) and auto-moving layouts (2.2.2). Enterprise wikis (Confluence, Notion, GitBook, Docusaurus) either lack backlinks/graph natively or bolt them on, and where they exist they must be permission-filtered per viewer, which matters for OneHub.
--- TENSIONS
 * Graph view: 'decoration you open once' (many forum users, PracticalPKM 'kinda useless' for global) versus 'diagnostic' (Holloran, TypeFire, Konik, Büge: orphans, hubs, clusters) versus 'most important pillar / spatial awareness' (a July 2024 forum advocate). Karpathy's gist sits on the pro side but frames it as viewing, with index.md and lint doing the operational work.
 * Local graph: 'almost magical' (a Substack PKM writer) and recommended by Ango/PracticalPKM versus 'a fancy backlink view' (forum user tobei) and 'doesn't work for my system' (Konik). The disagreement is about whether it adds anything beyond a good backlinks panel.
 * Backlinks: 'critical' and used constantly by the Obsidian CEO versus 'linking notes without connecting knowledge' with no reason to follow (Zettelkasten.de) versus noise/false positives for unlinked references (Logseq forum). LLM-authored links could fix the missing-context problem but also risk over-linking (Why Graph README calls default LLM-vault graphs a hairball).
 * Node-link versus matrix at scale: Ghoniem et al. find matrices win beyond about 20 vertices on most tasks; Okoe et al. (2019, larger sparse real-world data) find node-link better for sparse networks and matrices slower and less precise at edge-finding. The right answer depends on density and task, and a wiki link graph is usually sparse.
 * Built-in graph is enough versus not: Karpathy and most LLM-wiki plugins just use Obsidian's native graph; dsebastien, KnodeGraph (vendor), Why Graph and wiki-to-graph argue it lacks metadata colouring, typed edges, review tracking and frontier detection. Several of those authors sell or promote their own tool.
 * Scale ceilings: Obsidian moderator says nothing above about 25K files is practical, D3 canvas/WebGL hold 30 fps to about 5,000-7,000 nodes, but legibility complaints start at a few hundred nodes (Holloran, Why Graph) or 500 (KnodeGraph, vendor) and academic node-link readability declines from tens of vertices. Rendering and legibility ceilings differ by one to two orders of magnitude.
 * Where index.md stops working: Karpathy says about 100 sources / hundreds of pages; a secondary source says about 200 pages; another says degradation at 200K-300K tokens.
 * Human approval versus speed: lucasastorian/llmwiki (1.6k stars) deliberately has no pending-change step, the Obsidian plugin uses a reviewed: true flag, HN commenters demand mandatory human review before agent output is promoted, and OneHub's plan is diff approval by page-owners. Also HN: 'Everyone is writing. Nobody is reading.', a challenge to how much any navigation UI will actually be used by humans.
 * Search versus navigation: NN/g says search alone is not enough and browsing structure (breadcrumbs, hierarchy, TOC) still matters; PKM power-user evidence (Ango, PracticalPKM) shows quick switcher/command palette dominating. The two describe different user types (occasional employee readers versus daily authors), which is my reading, not a finding in either source.
 * Graph versus list for maintenance: MediaWiki, llmwiki-tooling and llm-wiki-starter treat orphans, dead ends and wanted pages as ranked lists; Obsidian users and Karpathy's gist point to visually spotting them in the graph.
--- IMPLICATIONS
 > INFERENCE - Order the navigation build by evidence, not by demo appeal: (1) fast search plus a keyboard quick-switcher/command palette, (2) a 'Referenced by' backlinks panel with a snippet of context, (3) hover/focus preview cards, (4) breadcrumbs and a right-rail table of contents with active-section highlight, (5) property-driven list views (type, owner, status, effective date, review state), (6) a local graph (depth 1-2) as a secondary panel on a page, (7) a global graph last, aimed at page-owners/admins as a wiki-health view, not as everyday navigation. Every source I found ranks the global graph below these for daily work.
 > INFERENCE - Make orphan, dead-end, wanted (linked-but-missing), broken-link, stale, and 'pending diff' pages ranked report lists first (the MediaWiki special-pages and llmwiki-tooling pattern), and let the graph highlight the same sets. The list is also the accessible alternative and the natural page-owner to-do queue.
 > INFERENCE - Backlinks and graph edges written by the agent should carry a stored reason (the sentence or a short 'why') and ideally a type, so the panel is not the context-free list Zettelkasten.de criticises and so reviewers can judge a proposed link in the diff. For regulatory content, typed edges such as supersedes / amends / implements / cites / contradicts are likely worth more than generic links (wiki-to-graph derives types from section headings; its licence is CC BY-NC-SA, so borrow the idea, not the code, unless legal clears it).
 > INFERENCE - Treat unlinked mentions as an agent job whose output lands in the normal human-approved diff (llmwiki-tooling 'links check/fix' is the precedent), rather than a separate noisy human panel; Logseq users' false-positive complaints argue for this.
 > INFERENCE - Encode workflow state on graph nodes, not just topology: approved / pending review / stale / draft, page type, owner, freshness. dsebastien's hollow-vs-solid reviewed fill with a coverage bar and nashsu's knowledge-gap insights are working models. Use shape/fill plus colour so colour is never the only channel.
 > INFERENCE - Backlinks, previews, search results and the graph must all be computed server-side per viewer. Notion hides or labels 'Private' backlinks and Confluence's link-graph app filters by view permission; Quartz-style shipping of a whole-site contentIndex.json to the browser would leak titles and links of pages a viewer cannot open. Return only nodes/edges the viewer may read, with at most an aggregate 'N restricted' marker.
 > INFERENCE - Expect the wiki to start at tens to low hundreds of pages, where a whole-graph view is at best borderline legible. Plan for: server-side filtering before layout (Obsidian's freeze bug), cached/stable layout positions (nashsu), community collapse or a clustered list past a few hundred nodes, and canvas/WebGL only if you ever pass roughly 2,000-5,000 nodes (benchmark thresholds). Rendering scale is not the likely constraint; readability is.
 > INFERENCE - Build the graph as progressive enhancement over a real, keyboard-operable list/tree with the same data: focusable nodes or list-driven focus, arrow-key traversal, a text summary of the neighbourhood, a non-drag way to pan/select (WCAG 2.5.7), layout that settles and stops (2.2.2) and honours prefers-reduced-motion, no colour-only encoding. Do not expect ARIA Graphics roles to describe a node-link graph. OneHub is internal, so this is craft and inclusion rather than a legal mandate, but the list fallback costs little and doubles as the review queue.
 > INFERENCE - Copy Wikipedia's hover-card mechanics for previews: configurable dwell delay, keyboard-focus trigger, tooltip ARIA semantics, one-click off switch, summaries from a dedicated lightweight endpoint rather than rendering the full page client-side. For the agent's answers, previews of the cited passage (deep link to the heading anchor) are the citation-trust equivalent.
 > INFERENCE - Reuse the same link graph the humans see for agent retrieval and for 'why was this page used' trails (nashsu 2-hop expansion, green-dalii Personalized PageRank). Showing the retrieval path in the answer would tie the navigation UI to the citation UI and let page-owners see which pages the agent leans on (hubs) and which it never touches (orphans).
 > INFERENCE - Add a 'questions the agent could not answer' list per space, feeding page-owners, modelled on GitBook's Content gaps. It is a maintenance signal with clearer action than any graph picture.
 > INFERENCE - Instrument navigation from day one (search queries, quick-switcher opens, backlink clicks, preview hovers, graph opens, orphan-list actions) because there is no public usage telemetry to lean on; decide on the global graph's future from OneHub's own numbers. Follow the repo's telemetry rules (docs/telemetry.md was not reviewed in this research).
 > INFERENCE - Contrast with lucasastorian/llmwiki, whose agent writes go live with no pending state: OneHub's diff-approval model means every navigation surface (backlinks, graph, search, previews, agent citations) needs a defined rule for pending and unapproved content. The safe default is that readers and the agent only ever see approved versions, while owners see pending badges.
