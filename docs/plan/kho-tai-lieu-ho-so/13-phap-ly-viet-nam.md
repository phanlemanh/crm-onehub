Vietnamese law and compliance for an internal AI knowledge base (Kho tai lieu), and the model-provider path (Vercel AI Gateway, region, retention), as of 2026-09-26. Research only, not legal advice.
--- SUMMARY
Method note: the WebSearch budget was exhausted (200/200), so no law-firm alerts, news or Vietnamese case commentary could be searched. Everything below comes from direct fetches of primary sources: the signed PDFs on chinhphu.vn (OCR-transcribed where scanned, key pages also read visually), provider documentation dated Aug-Sep 2026, and OneHub's own repo. Five headline results. (1) Sending any personal data to an overseas LLM API is a cross-border transfer by the plain text of PDPL Art 20(1)(a)-(c) and Decree 356/2025 Art 17(1); the dossier is due to the Ministry of Public Security's specialised agency within 60 days of the first transfer (PDPL Art 20(2), Decree Art 18(4)). The '45 days' figure most plausibly matches the agency's review windows of 15 + 30 days (Decree Art 18(5)-(6)), which is my inference, not a stated rule. The dossier is filed once for the entity's lifetime and updated every 6 months when changes occur (PDPL Art 20(3), 22). Small-enterprise opt-outs in PDPL Art 38 do NOT cover Art 20. (2) The only exemptions relevant to OneHub are narrow: storing your own employees' data on cloud (Art 20(6)(b)) and the Decree 356 Art 17(3) list; being a processor's customer or 'drafting only' is not an exemption, and only true de-identification (PDPL Art 2(1)) takes data outside the regime. (3) Financial and credit information of customers of banks, securities firms, insurers and payment intermediaries is sensitive data (Decree Art 4(1)(k)); it triggers extra duties (Art 4(2), 6(4), 7(2), 8) and I recommend keeping it out of overseas models. (4) The AI Law 134/2025/QH15 (effective 2026-03-01) applies to an internal assistant as a 'deployer' (possibly also a 'provider' if branded as OneHub's own); the 2027-03-01 and 2027-09-01 dates apply only to systems already operating before 2026-03-01 (Art 35), so a new KB assistant has no grace period. (5) No provider or gateway can pin inference in Vietnam or in Singapore/Tokyo/Jakarta: Vercel AI Gateway offers only us, eu or global; Anthropic first-party only us or global; OpenAI in-region processing only US/EU (Singapore and Japan are storage-only); Bedrock Claude Sonnet 5 single-region only in Melbourne; Vertex/Google Asia options could not be verified. OneHub's agent today calls the gateway with a bare model id and no providerOptions, so routing is the gateway default (any provider, any region, no ZDR). The biggest unresolved legal risks are whether a foreign LLM provider counts as a 'personal data processing service' needing a Vietnamese certificate (Decree Arts 21-25), what the new cybersecurity decrees of Aug-Sep 2026 say about localisation, and the unread penalty decrees 330/2026 and 363/2026.
--- TENSIONS
 * Filing clock: PDPL Art 20(2) and Decree 356 Art 18(4) say 60 days AFTER the first transfer (primary). Some summaries describe a 45-day process; the text has 15 + 30 days for the AGENCY review (Art 18(5)-(6)). Separately, Decree 165/2025 Art 12 for non-personal important data appears (OCR, ambiguous) to require sending the dossier about 15 days BEFORE processing, and prior approval for core data. Two regimes, two clocks; Decree 356 Art 42(3) and PDPL Art 5(4) make personal data follow the PDPL clock.
 * Overseas cloud: some law-firm summaries in the earlier research treated foreign cloud as unclear; the primary text is explicit: PDPL Art 20(1)(a),(c) and Decree Art 17(1)(a),(c) include storing on servers or cloud services of a foreign provider and using a foreign platform to process data collected in Vietnam. The only cloud exemption is employees' own data (Art 20(6)(b)).
 * AI Law dates: secondary sources give 2027-03-01 (general) and 2027-09-01 (finance, health, education). The primary text limits these to systems already operating before 2026-03-01; new systems must comply from launch. Also Art 6(2) names only health and education as essential fields while Art 35 names finance, health and education.
 * Anthropic retention statements differ: the API and data-retention page says conversation content is 'not retained by default' (except Covered Models needing 30 days), while the Privacy Center says API inputs and outputs are deleted within 30 days of receipt; both say flagged content may be kept up to 2 years. Treat default as up to 30 days unless a ZDR agreement is signed.
 * Bedrock: the abuse-detection page says inputs and outputs are not stored by default (ZOA and ZDR model) while listing retained-traffic exceptions for named OpenAI and Anthropic models; the retention page adds account modes that can make some models unavailable under 'none'.
 * Vercel's default ZDR statement (gateway deletes prompts and responses after completion) versus provider behaviour: Vercel says its retention does not constrain providers and that a provider's default policy may differ from the gateway agreement; also abuse or safety retention can fall outside a pinned region.
 * Sensitive-data scope: Decree 356 Art 4(1)(k) speaks of customers' financial information 'at' regulated financial institutions; a marketplace's own records of an individual's loan or income information may or may not be inside that wording; secondary commentary broadly says 'financial information is sensitive' without this limit.
 * Anonymisation: PDPL Art 2(1) says de-identified data is not personal data, but Art 12(1) says encrypted data still is, and the promised state technical regulations on de-identification do not appear to exist yet; commentary that treats pseudonymised data as safe is not supported by the text.
 * Provider-region claims: Vercel docs advertise regional residency, but only us/eu; Anthropic first-party us/global; Bedrock Claude Sonnet 5 only Melbourne single-region; OpenAI Singapore/Japan storage-only. Any claim of 'Asia region' for these models must be checked per model and endpoint.
--- IMPLICATIONS
 > DECISION TREE for which document classes may go to an overseas model (label each source doc at ingest; run the classifier before any overseas call). Node 1: public and personal-data-free (published laws, regulations, public templates) = Class P: send anywhere; no PDPL step. Node 2: internal and personal-data-free, not on the Prime Minister's important/core-data list (list status unverified), no third-party confidentiality clause forbidding it = Class I: may go to an overseas model through the gateway with zeroDataRetention, disallowPromptTraining and a provider allowlist; log finalProvider and inferenceEndpoint.geoRegion. Node 3: contains only basic personal data of staff or third parties (names, phones, emails, addresses in minutes or org charts) = Class B: default is REDACT irreversibly before sending (role tokens, no retained mapping); if identities are truly required, treat as a cross-border transfer needing the dossier and a contract, and prefer in-country processing. Node 4: sensitive personal data (customer financial or credit information and transaction history at banks, securities, insurers, payment intermediaries; bank or card data; ID-card images; health; biometrics; location; criminal; political or religious data) or bank-partner customer files = Class S: MUST STAY IN-COUNTRY (self-hosted or Vietnam-hosted model) unless irreversibly de-identified; if counsel approves an exception, require explicit consent flagged as sensitive, encryption and anonymisation, DPO sign-off, and the 72-hour breach runbook. Node 5: on the important/core-data list = Class D: core data never without prior approval (Decree 165 Art 12(5)); important data only after the Art 12 dossier. Node 6: trade secrets, investor or M&A material, source code, privileged legal advice = Class T: contractual not statutory limit; ZDR mandatory, otherwise keep in-country. Node 7: anything that could be a state secret = never.
 > TWO-LANE DESIGN reduces legal exposure: Lane A (ingest and drafting from raw Word, PDF, scans and minutes) is where personal data lives, so put the classifier and redactor in front of it and route only Class P/I/B-redacted text overseas; Lane B (answering staff) should read only approved wiki pages, which reviewers should sanitise of personal data as an approval criterion, so answers can use the ZDR-pinned overseas route. Make 'no personal data or redaction confirmed' an explicit checkbox in the page-owner diff review.
 > RECOMMENDED GATEWAY CONFIGURATION now (no code change needed for the first two): (1) enable the team-wide provider allowlist (Pro or Enterprise) restricted to vetted providers, for example anthropic, bedrock, vertex, azure, openai; (2) enable team-wide ZDR or set providerOptions.gateway.zeroDataRetention true plus disallowPromptTraining true through eve modelOptions.providerOptions (untested whether eve forwards gateway keys; test with a probe and read providerMetadata.gateway.routing); (3) set inferenceRegion to eu or us (pick one; neither is 'near Vietnam'; legal analysis does not require a destination-country test, but the dossier must assess the recipient's protection level); (4) avoid claude-fable-5 and 5.1 because they cannot be ZDR and would fail closed; (5) store routing metadata per call in the audit trail; (6) send a hashed safetyIdentifier, never names or emails.
 > FILINGS AND GOVERNANCE to do before launch: confirm whether OneHub already filed a cross-border dossier for the CRM (Neon, Vercel, other overseas SaaS); if not, the 60-day clock from first transfer may already have run since 2026-01-01; add the gateway, model provider and their sub-processors as recipients and update within the 6-month cycle; appoint DPO staff or hire a service unless OneHub is a small enterprise that does not process sensitive data or 100,000+ data subjects (and even then the cross-border dossier is still owed); add a Vietnam-law addendum (Decree 356 Art 12(2), 18(2)(b)) to provider paths; publish an AI-use notice and opt-out route where customer data can reach the model (Decree Art 10(3)); classify the assistant under the AI Law and label answers as AI-generated with citations.
 > AUDIT AND EVIDENCE design: append-only approval log with user identity, timestamp, diff hash, source hash and model routing metadata; export to write-once storage; keep at least 5 years for anything that also serves as AML, KYC or transaction evidence if OneHub is a reporting entity; keep consent proofs and dossier copies; keep incident register and processing logs. A click-to-approve is a valid electronic record (LET Art 8-11) but not a statutory e-signature (Art 22(4)); use a digital signature where law requires a signed document.
 > ANSWER-RELIANCE controls: every answer must cite the approved page and its version; staff procedure to verify before repeating to customers; retain the question, answer, cited page hash and user, because the business bears the burden of proving no fault to consumers (Consumer Law Art 69(2)) and answers Civil Code Art 600 for staff acts.
 > OPEN COST OF IN-COUNTRY ROUTE: for Class S and Class T, evaluate FPT AI Factory and GreenNode for open-weight models and confirm entity form, data centre, retention and Vietnamese-language quality; a Vietnamese-entity host could also address the Decree 356 Art 21-22 processing-service question if the model provider is treated as one.
 > DO NOT rely on the SME opt-out or on 'we only draft' as exemptions; do not assume a provider's global DPA satisfies Vietnamese contract requirements; do not describe region routing as Vietnam residency to regulators or customers.
