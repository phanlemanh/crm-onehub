====================================================================================================
Vietnamese-language document ingestion, retrieval and drafting quality for the OneHub Knowledge Base (Kho tai lieu): OCR and parsing, legacy encodings and normalisation, retrieval on Postgres, token cost, LLM reading and drafting, meeting ASR
--- SUMMARY
Method note: the WebSearch budget was already exhausted (200 of 200) when this follow-up started, so I worked from primary sources through WebFetch, the arXiv API, the Hugging Face API, GitHub, vendor docs, plus my own experiments. Those experiments were throwaway offline Postgres 16 (glibc) and Postgres 17 (musl) containers, iconv, and computations on a 71-document sample of the public vbpl.vn legal corpus. There are no blog or forum sources. The main result is that public Vietnamese evidence is thin and skewed. There is no public benchmark of real scanned Vietnamese official documents (cong van, quyet dinh, stamps, faint fax). The closest data are the multilingual parsing benchmarks MDPBench and SEA-Vision (both March 2026), which include Vietnamese pages of papers, books, magazines and newspapers, and CC-OCR v2 (May 2026), which tests stamp overlays but not per language. On these, frontier VLMs lead: Gemini-3-pro-preview scores 85.9 on Vietnamese in MDPBench, Claude-Sonnet-4.6 83.1 and GPT-5.2 82.1. Photographed pages cost 5 to 23 points depending on the model. Several popular OCR-specialist models collapse on Vietnamese: MonkeyOCR, MinerU and PP-StructureV3 score 28-53 in MDPBench, dots.mocr shows language drift (Vietnamese read as Chinese), and Google Document AI, Azure Document Intelligence and Mistral OCR have no independent Vietnamese numbers at all. Encoding is a smaller and more archival problem than expected: legacy TCVN3/VNI appears mainly in files from before about 2008, but a font name is a poor signal and mojibake is silent. The problem that hits every Vietnamese pipeline is tone-mark placement. In my sample of 71 legal documents, all were NFC, but old-style (hoa with mark on o) versus new-style (hoa with mark on a) placement is mixed (85% old / 15% new, 27 of 71 documents mix both). About 18% of sentence-length strings contain at least one syllable where the two styles differ, so an exact-quote check that ignores this will reject roughly 1 in 5 correct quotes whenever the model or OCR flips style. Postgres unaccent (tested on PG16 glibc and PG17 musl) handles d-bar and tone placement but not i/y variants (quy vs qui). It must run before to_tsvector, otherwise citations like 20/2026/ND-CP tokenize wrongly; decimal commas (7,5) split; and phrase queries match across sentence boundaries. For retrieval, the best independent evidence says: zero-shot bge-m3 is mediocre on Vietnamese legal sets (Zalo Legal 2021 MRR@10 0.64-0.68; TVPL 0.33), in-domain fine-tuning or hybrid plus reranking adds a lot (0.65 to 0.86 R@1), and a well-segmented BM25 is a strong baseline on small regulation corpora. I found no independent evidence for Qwen3-Embedding or jina v3/v4 on Vietnamese legal retrieval, for word segmentation effects, or for diacritic-free queries. Vietnamese costs roughly 1.3-1.5x English tokens on modern large-vocabulary tokenizers (GPT-4o family 1.29 tokens per syllable-word, 1.50x on FLORES) but 2-3.6x on older ones. Anthropic states Claude 4.7-and-later tokenizers give about 30% more tokens than earlier models, and no published Vietnamese measurement exists for current Claude or Gemini tokenizers. On generation, VietLegal / VLegal-Bench (arXiv 2512.14554, v5 April 2026) did not test 2026 frontier models: its newest are Claude Sonnet 4.5, GPT-4o and Gemini 2.5 Flash. The 16 of 23 zero conflict-detection F1 is largely a zero-shot label-collapse effect that few-shot prompting fixes. An error study of four 2024-vintage models found omitted exceptions in about 16-19% of responses. Meeting ASR has no public evidence on meeting-like Vietnamese speech for Gemini, FPT.AI or Viettel. What exists is PhoWhisper (VLSP2020 spontaneous task WER 26.7% by its authors) and Whisper large-v3 at 8.6-17.9% on read or web sets. Punctuation and casing drive most of the raw-WER gap. Everything labelled 'proposed' in the protocol thresholds below is my inference, anchored to the numbers cited.
--- TENSIONS
 * Who leads Vietnamese page parsing: MDPBench puts Gemini-3-pro clearly first among general VLMs (VI 85.9 vs Qwen3-VL-8B 73.4), while SEA-Vision has Qwen3-VL-32B (text NED 0.076) and Qwen2.5-VL-72B (0.069) ahead of Gemini 2.5 Pro (0.098) on Vietnamese text; model sizes and versions differ (8B vs 32B/72B), and the two benchmarks use different page types and metrics.
 * MDPBench ground truth was seeded by PaddleOCR-VL, dots.ocr and Qwen3-VL, yet Gemini-3-pro still wins; VMMU's OCR ground truth came from o3, which then scores best. Ground-truth circularity may inflate the models that helped build the labels.
 * Specialist OCR models are marketed as multilingual (PaddleOCR-VL '109 languages'), but both benchmarks show large per-model swings on Vietnamese: PaddleOCR-VL-1.5 has the best VI text crops (0.031) and yet PaddleOCR-VL v1 scores 66.6 overall, MonkeyOCR-pro-3B scores 40.2 with NED 0.72, and dots.mocr misreads Vietnamese as Chinese.
 * Dense versus lexical retrieval: on the 1.29M-article legal library dense beats BM25 (R@1 0.74 vs 0.65) and hybrid is better still; on a 294-article regulation set a segmented BM25 (68.75% top-1) beats untuned dense models (43-52%); on TVPL BM25 is very weak (MRR@10 21.6) and on Zalo 2021 it beats vietnamese-sbert; in the labour-law study hybrid RRF did not beat dense (0.350 vs 0.358). Corpus size, query style and model tuning decide, so no default can be inherited.
 * Domain fine-tuning helps and hurts: AITeamVN's bge-m3 fine-tune gains about 16 points Acc@1 on Zalo Legal 2021 (0.568 to 0.727 by its own card) but drops from 39.84 to 34.18 on VN-MTEB retrieval; vietnamese-bi-encoder scores 80.7 MRR@10 on Zalo21 but 25.4 on VN-MTEB retrieval; possible training overlap with Zalo makes the gain uncertain.
 * VietLegal reports 16 of 23 models with 0.00 conflict-detection F1 and calls it inability; the same paper shows few-shot prompting turns GPT-4o and Gemini-2.5-Flash into balanced detectors, and zero-shot models were predicting 'no conflict' almost always, so the headline is mostly a prompting and label-collapse effect, not a fixed ceiling.
 * Retrieval is not uniformly helpful in VietLegal: it lifts article lookup by 10-22 points but lowers multi-article reasoning and court-decision prediction slightly, while a domain-adapted 32B model without retrieval beats GPT-4o on several reasoning tasks.
 * Tone-mark placement: sources call it a debated convention; the vbpl.vn-derived sample is 85% old style but 27 of 71 documents mix both, so any single canonical form is a policy choice, not a fact of the language; i/y variants (ly/li) were consistent in that sample but are not guaranteed in staff input.
 * Token premium depends on the denominator: 1.50x on FLORES sentences vs 1.29 tokens per whitespace word on GPT-4o; per-word numbers look cheap because Vietnamese words are single syllables and Vietnamese needs more words per meaning. Claude 2.1 and Claude-3 numbers (3.56x, 2.89 tokens/word) are not evidence about current Claude models, whose tokenizer Anthropic says changed at 4.7.
 * Vendor multilingual claims versus independent results: Anthropic's multilingual table omits Vietnamese, Mistral omits it, and Azure/Google list it only in language tables, while CC-OCR shows Vietnamese trailing French by 10-17 points for the same VLM in 2024.
 * ASR numbers are not comparable across sources: PhoWhisper-small 33.90 raw vs 8.97 normalised WER in the same paper, PhoWhisper-large 26.68 on VLSP2020 Task-2 by its authors, and Whisper large-v3 8.59-17.94 on read or web sets in another paper; nobody reports meeting speech.
 * viparse's author states 0.986-0.999 diacritic accuracy but also that no other tool has been run on the corpus and that some measurements were circular or corrected after scorer bugs; treat the direction (conversion matters) as credible and the decimals as unverified.
--- IMPLICATIONS
 > PIPELINE (a1) BORN-DIGITAL PDF: always keep the page images; extract the text layer, then run a per-page trust test before believing it: reject when any of U+FFFD, private-use code points or '(cid:' appear, when legacy-mojibake characters sit between ASCII letters, or when the diacritic-bearing syllable share is far below the 0.815-0.919 range seen in real legal text (my sample). Text that fails goes to the OCR path. Legacy TCVN3 pages: convert lowercase with glibc iconv -f TCVN5712-1 (verified) or viparse (VNI, uppercase headings), only after the text test says it is not already Unicode; treat a font name as a hint only (44% false positives). Then NFC; keep the stored text as authored; compute a separate quote_key (NFC, tone-placement canonicalised, NBSP and zero-width removed, whitespace collapsed, quotes and dashes unified). Tables: run a VLM page parse and route every table with numbers to human review (MDPBench table TEDS 75.9 digital; SEA-Vision VI 0.92-0.94 best case).
 > PIPELINE (a2) SCANS, PHOTOS, STAMPED AND FAX PAGES: render at 300 dpi or more without aggressive binarisation (proposed; keep colour so red seals stay separable). Engine A: a current Gemini Pro class VLM (MDPBench VI 85.9, smallest photographed drop 5.3; CC-OCR v2 average 94.4) with a 'transcribe verbatim, keep every diacritic and digit, do not correct, mark [?] for illegible' prompt. Engine B, independent: self-hostable PaddleOCR-VL (Apache-2.0; VI 78.9, SEA-Vision VI TEDS 0.927) or Qwen3-VL-32B (Apache-2.0; SEA-Vision VI text NED 0.076). Do not use MonkeyOCR, MinerU or PP-StructureV3 for Vietnamese (scores 28-53; NED 0.28-0.72); if dots.ocr or dots.mocr is used, add a language-drift check. Disagreement gate: flag any block where A and B differ by more than about 5% edit distance or where any digit, date, amount, name or article number differs, and show the page image beside the text for a human. Never let an LLM 'clean up' OCR silently (seal hallucination is a documented failure; a rule-based repair also made Tesseract results worse). Open question for the owner: whether internal contracts and personnel documents may go to a cloud VLM at all; the self-hosted Engine B alone costs roughly 6-8 points on MDPBench VI versus the best cloud model.
 > PIPELINE (a3) DOCX: parse the OOXML directly (w:t text, w:ins/w:del for tracked changes so the diff shows accepted and rejected views), rebuild Dieu/khoan/diem numbering from the numbering definitions because auto-numbers are not in the text (proposed), normalise to NFC, check runs that name .VnTime/.VnArial/VNI fonts by looking at the text not the font, and handle w:softHyphen (in legacy TCVN3 it is the letter u-horn). Convert legacy .doc through LibreOffice first and re-run the text checks. No independent Vietnamese evidence exists for this path, so it needs the local test set.
 > PIPELINE (a4) XLSX: read typed cell values and number formats with a spreadsheet reader instead of exporting text; keep numbers as structured values because the Postgres parser splits decimal commas ('7,5' becomes '7' and '5') and Vietnamese thousand-dot numbers differ from English; render each sheet to Markdown with its header row and flag merged cells and formulas whose cached value differs. No evidence found beyond my Postgres probes; validate on the 4 sample workbooks.
 > PIPELINE (a5) MEETING AUDIO AND TRANSCRIPTS: candidates are PhoWhisper-large (BSD-3; author WER 26.68% on spontaneous VLSP2020 Task-2), Whisper large-v3 (8.59-17.94% on FLEURS, Common Voice, GigaSpeech 2 in a third-party table) and cloud engines (Azure 11.78, Google USM 13.03 average on the same sets); none has meeting-audio evidence. Add punctuation and casing restoration (raw-vs-normalised gap 33.9 vs 9.0 WER) and mark transcripts as leads: numbers, names and decisions need human confirmation before they become a quoted page source.
 > SEARCH DESIGN (from my own probes and the retrieval literature): store NFC text and a separate search key; apply unaccent() and lowercase to the text BEFORE to_tsvector('simple', ...) (not as a dictionary after the parser, which breaks 20/2026/ND-CP); keep document numbers in an exact-match column and numbers in structured columns; use phraseto_tsquery for multi-syllable terms and remember it still crosses sentence boundaries (split into sentence-level rows or add a boundary token); add pg_trgm for typos and i/y variants (unaccent does not merge quy/qui); hybrid retrieval with pgvector plus RRF (RoPA Manager used k=60), then a cross-encoder rerank of the top 30-50; chunk by Dieu/khoan structure with chunks of roughly 250-512 tokens (papers used under 256, about 230 average, 1,024 with overlap; none tested which is best). Start embeddings with bge-m3 (MIT) and compare multilingual-e5-large-instruct (MIT) and Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B/8B (Apache-2.0); avoid jina v3/v4 and jina rerankers for commercial internal use unless licensed (CC-BY-NC). Do not assume community Vietnamese fine-tunes generalise: they gain 15-20 points on Zalo/TVPL but lose on VN-MTEB, and overlap with the training data is possible.
 > QUOTE AND DIFF DESIGN: compare quotes on quote_key, never on raw strings: without canonicalisation about 18% of sentence-length quotes in my legal sample contain a syllable whose spelling depends on tone-placement style, so a style flip would cause about 1 in 5 false rejections. Show tone-style, NFC/NFD and NBSP-only changes as non-semantic in the owner's diff, and keep exact-match strictness for digits, dates and article numbers. Article, clause and document numbers in answers should be copied from structured page metadata, not generated (VietLegal documents invented legislation and sub-article granularity errors).
 > GENERATION DESIGN: ground every answer on retrieved approved pages, request explicit conditions and exceptions as a separate checklist (2024 models omitted exceptions in about 16-19% of responses), use a few examples in prompts for contradiction or conflict questions (few-shot removed the zero-F1 collapse in VietLegal), let multi-article questions retrieve several times rather than once (retrieval helped article lookup by 10-22 points but hurt multi-article reasoning slightly), and prefer reasoning-enabled frontier models for policy reading (VMMU: thinking models +20 points on Vietnamese multimodal exams; text-only legal transfer is my inference).
 > TOKEN AND COST PLANNING: budget 1.3-1.5x English tokens on GPT-4o-class or Qwen3-class tokenizers and up to 2-3x on older tokenizers until measured; for Claude use the free count_tokens endpoint against the exact model id (Claude 4.7+ is about 30% higher than earlier models per Anthropic), for Gemini use its count endpoint, and record tokens per Vietnamese syllable for your own pages. Full page images cost differently from text, so price OCR per page separately (Mistral OCR 3 lists $2 per 1,000 pages as a reference point).
 > PROTOCOL STEP E0, DATA (30-50 documents): 8 born-digital PDFs (include 2 old files with legacy fonts if any exist), 8 scans or photos (3 clean, 2 phone photos, 2 with red stamp and signature over text, 1 faint fax), 6 DOCX (2 with tracked changes, 1 with auto-numbered Dieu), 4 XLSX, 3 meetings of about 10 minutes, plus 4-6 documents with tables. Freeze the set before any tool is run.
 > PROTOCOL STEP E1, LABELS: (a) human-typed transcripts of 15 pages typed from the image, never from an OCR output (avoids the circular ground truth seen in MDPBench, VMMU and viparse-corpus), with every number, date, amount, name, document number and Dieu/khoan/diem marked as a critical field, and table cells marked; (b) 50 questions each with gold page(s), a gold verbatim quote of one or two sentences, the list of must-mention conditions or exceptions, the exact numeric answer, and an answerable or unanswerable flag (at least 10 unanswerable); (c) each question in 3 forms: accented, diacritics stripped (10 typed by staff), and Telex-typo or abbreviation form (QD, TT, ND-CP); (d) 20 quotes duplicated in the opposite tone-placement style and in NFD; (e) human transcripts for the 3 meetings with numbers and names marked.
 > PROTOCOL STEP E2, OCR AND PARSING (proposed thresholds): per page report CER, diacritic accuracy (share of ground-truth diacritic-bearing characters returned exactly), critical-field exact match, and table cell accuracy. Pass for clean scans: diacritic accuracy 0.98 or higher and CER 3% or lower. Pass for photos, stamped pages and fax: CER 8% or lower, and after the dual-engine gate the pages NOT flagged must have critical-field error 1% or lower. The gate must flag at least 95% of pages that contain any critical-field error. Any silent number or date error on an unflagged page fails the stage. Anchors: closed VLM CER 4.1-6.0% on clean exam images (VMMU), VI text edit distance 0.03-0.05 on digital crops (MDPBench), photographed drop 5-23 points, Tesseract self-report 0.973.
 > PROTOCOL STEP E3, TEXT-LAYER TRUST AND ENCODING: run the trust test on all born-digital and DOCX files; pass when every legacy-encoded or mark-stripped page is caught (0 misses) with at most 5% false alarms, and converted text reaches diacritic accuracy 0.98 or higher against the typed transcript. Log the share of files needing conversion in the real corpus so the team knows how big the archival problem is for OneHub.
 > PROTOCOL STEP E4, QUOTE CHECK: 100% of quotes the agent emits must be substrings of the approved page's quote_key; report the raw-string mismatch rate without canonicalisation (my sample predicts about 18% of sentence-length quotes if the style differs) and require 0 false accepts on 20 seeded mutants (one changed digit, dropped qualifier word, swapped syllable, changed tone).
 > PROTOCOL STEP E5, RETRIEVAL: on the 50 questions and 3 query forms compare at page level (a) Postgres simple+unaccent BM25 with ts_rank_cd, (b) plus pg_trgm, (c) bge-m3 dense, (d) hybrid RRF k=60, (e) hybrid plus cross-encoder, (f) mE5-large-instruct and Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B in place of bge-m3. Pass: accented Recall@5 0.90 or higher, Recall@10 0.95 or higher, MRR@10 0.75 or higher; the no-diacritic form within 10 points relative of the accented form and the Telex-typo form within 15 points. With n=50 the 95% interval is roughly 10-13 points, so use paired bootstrap and treat differences under 15 points as ties; also run the retrieval stage on an OCR-derived copy of the scans to measure how much OCR error costs.
 > PROTOCOL STEP E6, ANSWERS AND DIFFS: citation correctness (cited page contains the gold quote) 95% or higher; must-mention conditions recalled 95% or higher (2024 baseline 81-84%); zero invented article, clause or document numbers; numeric answers 100% exact; correct refusal on unanswerable questions 90% or higher; a Vietnamese-speaking reviewer samples 5,000 words for diacritic slips, mixed Vietnamese-English terminology and wrong legal terms with a limit of 1 per 500 words. Diff review: seed 20 drafts with one planted error each (dropped qualifier, changed number, wrong article reference, tone-style flip, NFC/NFD change): reviewers must catch 90% of the real errors and see 0 cosmetic-only alarms.
 > PROTOCOL STEP E7, TOKENS, ASR AND DATABASE: count the same 10 Vietnamese pages and their English translations on every candidate tokenizer and report tokens per Vietnamese syllable and the Vietnamese/English ratio; for the 3 meetings report raw WER and normalised WER, number/date/name error counts, and pass only if normalised WER is 15% or lower, otherwise label transcripts as leads only; replay my 8 Postgres probes (unaccent of d-bar, NFD versus NFC lexemes, i/y variants, citation tokenization, decimal comma, NBSP, phrase across sentence, unaccent order) on the actual Neon version and require the same outcomes.
 > TOP RISKS: (1) silent number or diacritic errors from OCR or a broken text layer reaching the wiki with no reviewer seeing the page image; (2) exact-quote check failing or, worse, being loosened because of tone-placement or NFC/NFD noise; (3) unaccent-based search returning plausible but wrong pages for diacritic-free queries (about 34% of unaccented forms are ambiguous in my sample) with no measured precision; (4) Postgres tokenization quietly splitting citations, decimals and phrases; (5) frontier-model behaviour on Vietnamese policy text is measured only on 2024-2025 models, so recency blindness, dropped exceptions and invented article numbers must be tested on the exact model chosen; (6) stamped and faint documents have no public benchmark, and CC-OCR v2 documents seal hallucination; (7) sending confidential documents to cloud OCR and LLMs; (8) licence traps (jina non-commercial, AGPL PhoBERT-v2, CC BY-NC SEA-Vision, MinerU AGPL) and a single-maintainer dependency for legacy-encoding conversion.
--- OPEN
 ? Method limits: WebSearch was exhausted before this task started, so blogs, forums and vendor case studies were not searched; sources are arXiv, Hugging Face, GitHub, vendor docs and my own experiments. Several web facts came through page summarisers (Google Document AI languages, Mistral pages, VMLU and SEA-HELM leaderboards, PaddleOCR README, ALQAC and Docling pages) and are only partly verified. The ALQAC 2024/2025 results, and the ViLLM-Eval leaderboard were not obtained.
 ? No public benchmark exists for real scanned Vietnamese official documents (cong van, quyet dinh, thong tu, contracts) with red seals, signatures, faint fax or phone photos; MDPBench, SEA-Vision, CC-OCR and VMMU use papers, books, magazines, newspapers, exams and word crops. Whether an OneHub-style stamped scan reaches the cited numbers is unknown.
 ? No independent Vietnamese accuracy exists for Google Document AI, Azure Document Intelligence Read/Layout, Mistral OCR 3, Tesseract vie, VietOCR on modern text, Docling with Vietnamese OCR, PaddleOCR-VL-1.6, PP-OCRv6, DeepSeek-OCR-2, GLM-OCR beyond MDPBench, Gemini 3.1 or Claude Opus 4.6 and later on Vietnamese pages.
 ? No measurement of diacritic-free query behaviour for bge-m3, multilingual-e5, Qwen3-Embedding, jina or rerankers; no ablation of word segmentation for BM25 or dense retrieval; no independent legal benchmark including Qwen3-Embedding or jina v3/v4; no retrieval-quality measurement of the Postgres simple+unaccent+trigram configuration for Vietnamese.
 ? My Postgres and normalisation results come from PG16 (glibc) and PG17 (musl) containers and a 71-document non-random sample of one portal's Unicode-era HTML; production Neon version, collation and corpus (including Word-exported PDFs and scans) may differ. Rates of legacy encodings and tone-style mixing in OneHub's own files are unknown. I could not verify from a primary source whether the Vietnamese administrative-format rules (Decree 30/2020 or Circular 01/2011) require Unicode Times New Roman; that is from memory only. I did not read the statute text of Article 15 of the IP Law myself.
 ? Current tokenizers of Claude 4.6 to 5.x and Gemini 3.x on Vietnamese were not measured (Anthropic's only statement is about 30% more tokens from 4.7); no published cost or context measurement on Vietnamese legal text exists. count_tokens on the actual model ids would answer this in minutes.
 ? No 2026 frontier model (GPT-5.x, Gemini 3.x, Claude 4.6 and later) has an independent Vietnamese legal reading, drafting or hallucination evaluation that I could find; diacritic errors, dropped qualifiers, invented article numbers and mixed Vietnamese-English terminology are unmeasured for them. VLegal-Bench and the 2604.16270 study use 2024-2025 models.
 ? Meeting audio: no public WER for Gemini, gpt-4o-transcribe, FPT.AI or Viettel on Vietnamese, none on meeting speech with overlaps, numbers and names, no diarization or inverse text normalisation accuracy for Vietnamese numbers and dates, and no measure of how ASR errors propagate into wiki drafts.
 ? Data-governance questions the research cannot answer: whether internal documents can go to cloud OCR and LLM APIs, and how licences (CC BY-NC SEA-Vision, AGPL PhoBERT-v2 and MinerU, CC-BY-NC jina) apply to a single-tenant internal tool.
