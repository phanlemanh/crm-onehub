====================================================================================================
Agentic knowledge base patterns in production: harness engineering (AGENTS.md/CLAUDE.md as living documents), memory-engineering repos, cross-session handoffs, and how teams keep an agent-written knowledge base trustworthy over months
--- SUMMARY
The New Stack's "6 agentic knowledge base patterns" (Feb 2026) is a survey of vendor and enterprise interviews, and every one of its six patterns describes a knowledge base that agents READ and that people or pipelines curate. None describes the OneHub model, where an agent drafts and a human owner approves each diff. The nearest first-party production evidence is elsewhere. OpenAI's harness-engineering team keeps repo docs current with linters, CI freshness checks and a recurring "doc-gardening" agent. Every's compound-engineering plugin runs a capture skill plus a refresh skill with keep/update/consolidate/replace/delete outcomes and a `retire_when` field. LangChain's OpenWiki tracks per-claim source evidence and re-verifies only what drifted. Anthropic's Managed Agents memory stores add immutable versions and read-only mounts. Lopopolo's harness-engineering repo sketches a "context sidecar" with an isolated staging area and a trusted publisher identity, but that design is labelled proposed, not proven. The strongest recurring lesson is that prose rules and LLM self-checks are not enough over months. Trust comes from deterministic mechanisms: verbatim-quote verification against source, source-version pointers that trigger re-verification, immutable versions, explicit trust tiers, expiry or retirement conditions, health telemetry on the pipeline itself, and golden-question evals. The main failure modes named in sources are error compounding, silent staleness, summarisation dropping exact wording (compaction erased 47% of safety rules after one compaction round in one measurement, 90% after five), reviewer fatigue, harness bloat that costs more than it helps (ETH Zurich: over 20% more inference cost with no general success gain), and silent death of the memory pipeline itself. Evidence quality is uneven. Several strong sources are N=1 self-reports (SIx Harness), vendor blogs (Mintlify, agentmemory) or 13-star indie projects, and no source gives months-long precision numbers for an agent-maintained, human-approved wiki at company scale. The web-search budget (200 calls) ran out mid-session, so Notion/Guru/Confluence-style "verified page" workflows and any Vietnamese-language evidence were not researched.
--- TENSIONS
 * Encode more into the repo vs. carry less: OpenAI and Lopopolo push more context, docs trees, quality grades and golden principles into the repository, while ETH Zurich (context files: no general success gain, +20% cost) and Marmelab ('reduce the harness to the strict minimum', median root file 123 lines vs OpenAI's ~100) say every instruction has a cost. The two are reconcilable only by the ETH distinction that instructions are followed while repository overviews are not helpful, and by evals per addition.
 * Who should write the harness: OpenAI's Codex wrote even the first AGENTS.md and most docs; Marmelab argues (as opinion) that a human should build the harness because an agent that needs supervision cannot decide the supervision it needs, yet cites an ablation (NLAH, arXiv 2603.25723) where a self-improving harness gained 4.8 and 2.7 points on two benchmarks. Marmelab also reports that in 52 of 79 repos the harness is more AI-written than the code it governs.
 * Automerge and minimal gates vs. mandatory human approval: OpenAI runs few blocking gates and automerges many doc-gardening PRs ('corrections are cheap') but calls that irresponsible at low throughput and still gates releases with a human smoke test; OpenWiki ships an optional auto-merge workflow but tells users to keep branch protection; critics (innobu, Proudfrog) say hallucinated facts compound and critical pages need PR-style human sign-off. The 'corrections are cheap' premise is the crux, and a wrong regulatory answer is plausibly not cheap (my inference).
 * Reviewer/evaluator agents: Anthropic finds a separate skeptical evaluator far easier to tune than self-critique (while noting it stays lenient toward LLM output); Lopopolo found reviewer agents caused thrash until told to bias toward merging; the NLAH ablation reports a second reviewer agent lowered success by 8% on one benchmark; Marmelab's own reviewer role often helps. GitBook and Mintlify sell the agent-as-reviewer model. Evidence is mixed and benchmark-specific.
 * Curate-first vs. capture-everything memory: claude-mem and MemPalace advocate automatic capture (MemPalace explicitly refuses to use an LLM to decide what to keep), while SIx Harness rejects roughly 62% of captured decisions and 75% of dead-ends and argues an ingest-everything channel becomes noise. Neither camp has controlled long-term comparisons; the SIx authors themselves note the counter-position is a net-cost thesis they did not measure.
 * LLM lint vs. deterministic lint, and lint-time vs. write-time contradiction handling: Karpathy's gist and Hermes rely on the LLM to lint and flag contradictions; skeptics say the same model class will certify its own errors; SIx ('model never judges its own memory') and MindBase/Cortex verify quotes in code; crajah argues contradictions must be resolved at write time via declared exclusive predicate groups because lint-time detection is quadratic and needs another LLM call. This trades away generality (you must declare mutual exclusion up front).
 * Summarised synthesis vs. verbatim text: the LLM Wiki premise is compiled synthesis, but compaction studies show summarising drops exact-wording rules (53% survive one round, 10% after five) and Cortex/MindBase only prove that quoted text exists in a source, not that the synthesis follows from it. For legal or financial text the synthesis layer likely needs pinned verbatim clauses (my inference).
 * Scale limits of index.md: Karpathy says the index works to roughly hundreds of pages; LLM Wiki v2 says 100-200 then hybrid search; Hermes adds a topic map past 200 entries; critics claim collapse past ~1,000 files (unmeasured); Cortex dropped ranking over generated one-line summaries in favour of BM25 over full text. No source gives measured thresholds for a company corpus.
 * Continuity by compaction vs. by artifacts: Anthropic recommends combining compaction with a memory tool, but also found compaction insufficient and used resets plus files; Pocock's handoff docs say /compact is the default and handoff is for travel; Marmelab notes the 'fresh context every loop' variant rests on one uncontrolled toy run and that what helps is that work is written to files, not the wipe. Harness assumptions also expire with models (context resets became 'dead weight' with Opus 4.5).
 * Passive documentation vs. active loading: Stripe finds agents ignore passive docs and soft warnings, while the wiki pattern depends on agents choosing to read pages. Both OpenWiki (managed instruction block plus MCP search/read tools) and Every (discoverability check) address this by wiring the knowledge into loaded context or a tool, not just placing files in a folder.
 * Repo as the single system of record vs. keep truth with its owner: OpenAI pushes Slack and doc knowledge into the repo so the agent can see it; Lopopolo's later docs warn against creating second stale copies of live records and prefer connectors and pinned projections.
 * Ownership and contribution friction: LinkedIn lets teams ship local playbooks 'without lengthy review' to scale contribution, while the OneHub-style model, critics' advice and the Cortex/Link designs gate everything through review. LinkedIn's playbooks are human-authored, so its low-review approach does not carry over to agent-drafted regulatory content (my inference).
--- IMPLICATIONS
 > [Inference] Model the KB as three stores with different trust: (1) immutable raw sources (regulation PDFs, SOPs, minutes) with a content hash and version; (2) an untrusted candidate/staging area where the drafting agent proposes claim-and-evidence diffs; (3) published approved pages. The employee-facing agent mounts only store 3, read-only, pinned to one immutable published revision per conversation. This combines Lopopolo's sidecar boundary, OpenWiki's evidence pointers, Managed Agents read_only mounts and Hermes raw sha256. Because the CRM already puts intelligence in apps/agent and lets the API only write AgentTask rows, drafting fits there and publication should be a separate code path/identity.
 > [Inference] Enforce 'answer only from approved pages' by tool permission and retrieval filter, not by prompt text. Sources show prose rules are rarely enforced (4.4% of CLAUDE.md security rules had a real control) and that a read_write store can be poisoned by prompt injection. The staging/candidate channel must be unreachable from the answering agent's retrieval path.
 > [Inference] Make every claim carry a source pointer with source version, e.g. doc id + version hash + page/clause, and re-verify only claims whose source version changed (OpenWiki pattern, extended from repo lines to documents). A new version of a regulation or policy then produces a small diff limited to affected claims and a to-do list for the page owner instead of a full rewrite.
 > [Inference] Put deterministic checks around the LLM, since sources warn that a same-model lint tends to certify its own text: verify that every quoted passage exists verbatim in the cited source version (Cortex, MindBase), extract numbers, dates, thresholds and article numbers by script and compare them, and fail the draft when a claim lacks a citation. For computed figures (fees, commissions, rates) follow OKF Attested Computation: the agent fills parameters of a reviewed formula and never writes the formula.
 > [Inference] Adopt OKF-style frontmatter as the page schema, which maps one-to-one onto the human-approval model: generated {by, at}; verified with a `human:<id>` actor recorded on owner approval; status draft/stable/deprecated; stale_after as a UTC-offset timestamp (Vietnam is UTC+7, and OKF's own bug report shows date-only values are ambiguous); sources with version. Derive trust tier from verified; the answering agent should warn or refuse on stale or unverified pages and always cite page + revision. Treat OKF itself as young (v0.2 landed July 2026, v0.3 proposed) and keep the mapping thin.
 > [Inference] Never leave superseded regulation text retrievable as if current. The Memory Trust Gap and TEPA results say stale notes with a newer-looking cue beat authoritative evidence and that append-only memory can score below no memory. Use explicit supersession (close, timestamp, keep for audit, exclude from default retrieval) and store both the effective date of the rule and the date the KB learned it (crajah's 'two clocks'). Consider Every's rule that git history is the archive rather than an _archived/ folder that pollutes search.
 > [Inference] Protect reviewer attention, the scarce resource in Lopopolo's account. Keep diffs small and claim-scoped, put the source quote next to each changed claim, allow auto-accept only for validator-verified mechanical changes (link fixes, formatting, date bumps) and require owner approval for anything semantic or policy-bearing. Track approval rate, edit-before-approve rate and time-to-review per owner; SIx's 59/154 and 47/186 survival rates show a high reject rate is normal, while a near-100% approve rate on fast reviews would suggest rubber-stamping (Marmelab cites a study where 93% of permission prompts were approved).
 > [Inference] Give agent capture an admission gate like Every's (non-obvious, durable, material, with a 'narrowest durable home' check) and default to rejecting. Do not file every answered question or meeting back as a page automatically; SIx and the OpenAI data agent (user-confirmed memory saves) both keep a human or explicit gate on what becomes durable.
 > [Inference] Build the maintenance loop from day one, not later: scheduled doc-gardening (OpenAI), a refresh pass with keep/update/consolidate/replace/delete outcomes (Every), `retire_when` for pages that hold only while an outside condition holds (e.g. a circular pending replacement), and `stale_after` per page type (policies slow, rates and campaigns fast). Non-interactive refresh should only apply unambiguous fixes and mark ambiguous pages stale with a reason, opening a review item for the page owner.
 > [Inference] Monitor the pipeline itself, because the meta-failure is silent death (SIx: 84 of 85 failures came in the first three weeks; XBlueSky: a payload drift produced unusable records for two weeks unnoticed). Track: last successful ingest per source, count of sources changed but not yet re-verified, drafts waiting for review by age, pages past stale_after, retrieval index freshness, and an end-to-end canary question run daily. Alerts must be actionable or people learn to ignore them.
 > [Inference] Build a golden-question eval set from real employee questions (rules, thresholds, procedure steps) with expected page and quote, run it on every publication and daily in production (OpenAI data-agent evals). Test both directions: questions that must be answered and questions the KB must refuse because the page is stale, missing or unverified (Marmelab/Anthropic: one-sided evals give one-sided optimisation). Ablate: check that a page actually improves answers before keeping it, since ETH Zurich found context files often add cost without improving success.
 > [Inference] Keep live CRM facts (deals, contacts, prices, inventory) out of the wiki and reach them through tools, following Lopopolo's 'keep each truth with its owner' and OpenAI's runtime-context layer. The wiki should hold policies, SOPs, decisions, definitions and meeting outcomes, plus canonical metric definitions to prevent the 'three definitions of revenue' problem Amazon's Kovi describes.
 > [Inference] Keep the developer harness (this repo's AGENTS.md/CLAUDE.md, ~100-line map style) and the business KB as separate systems with different owners. Sources show harness files should stay short, tested and pruned (Marmelab, ETH), while the business KB needs owners, versions and approvals. Do not let the KB agent write into AGENTS.md except in a managed block, as OpenWiki does, and give each control or KB rule a recorded reason and expiry.
 > [Inference] Give each page an owner and distribute ownership by domain (LinkedIn's central vs local split; TNS 'distributed ownership'), and put page versions in Postgres with immutable version rows, content-hash preconditions on approval (Managed Agents' content_sha256 pattern) and an audit of who approved which revision. This also answers the enterprise critics' points about git history not being an audit log, and about concurrent agent writes.
 > [Inference] Test Vietnamese text handling early. Link 3.0's report that accented Latin ('déploiement' vs 'deploiement') and non-Latin scripts were silently unfindable, and that non-Latin titles collapsed to one filename, is a concrete class of bug; Vietnamese tone marks need Unicode normalisation (NFC/NFD), diacritic-insensitive matching as an option, and slug rules that keep Vietnamese titles distinct. None of the researched sources evaluated Vietnamese.

 ? Lopopolo's context sidecar with publisher identity is explicitly described as 'proposed'. I found no report that anyone runs it in production or what its reviewer load looks like.

 ? Handling of Vietnamese text (tokenisation, diacritics, mixed Vietnamese/English legal terms) in BM25/embedding retrieval is not evidenced in any source; Link 3.0's accent/non-Latin bug is the only indirect signal.
